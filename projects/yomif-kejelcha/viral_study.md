## 2. How the best sports-story videos are built (from transcripts)

### Video 1: "The Greatest Athlete To Never Win: Asafa Powell" (Jumpman, 428K, 26 min)

- **Hook (0:00-0:31):** opens on **Powell's own voice, no narrator**: "99% of the time at a championship I was never at my best… it felt like somebody had a voodoo doll". It goes straight into the 2012 fall ("when I got to like 40 m and I felt myself going to the ground"), then "you have to win… there's no way you can lose".
  - So the cold open is **3 soundbites stitched** into one confession before any narration.
- **Thesis line at 1:01:** "it's as if he ran stuck in a paradox… World Records… except for the most important three days of the year." Then: "his curse was to be the world's fastest man who couldn't win."
- **Structure:** strictly chronological, **one chapter per year** (2003 … 2015), shown as year cards in the timestamps. Each chapter follows the same loop:
  1. Build-up form ("9.91, 21 years old")
  2. Championship disappointment
  3. **"…and less than 2 weeks later he'd run 9.74"**
- That loop is the **pattern break that repeats on purpose**: the irony of a world record right after every failed final. Viewers start predicting it, and that keeps them watching.
- **Soundbites:** live commentary plays raw over every race ("Powell takes it… 9.91!"). The narrator pauses and the race call carries the moment.
  - One devastating interview line is held back to the 2007 chapter ("when I saw I wasn't in gold medal contention I gave up in the middle of the race"). The narrator then adds one sentence: "There is nothing to break down about this statement."
- **Numbers as the payoff:** the closing stat compares his 125-final average (9.956) with his championship-final average, which proves the thesis with data. It ends on "4 world records, most sub-10s ever… two bronze medals."
- **What we take:** the confession cold open, the paradox thesis line in the first 60 s, the repeat "…and days later" irony beat, and the stat that closes the case.

### Video 2: "The Tragic End of Lillian Board" (Athletes Archives, 280K, 29x on a 1.6K channel)

- **Hook:** a quote, then "There was a time when Britain believed it had found its next Olympic legend… Then everything changed. … Lillian Board was dead. She was only 22."
  - **The ending is given away in the first 30 s**, then it asks "**Why? What really happened?**" The open loop is the *how*, not the *what*.
- **Near-miss beat (4:02-4:33):** "For a moment, it looked as though she had done it… Besson crossed first in 52.03. Lillian followed in 52.12… a defeat by less than a tenth of a second." Then the reframe: "The question people kept asking wasn't 'How brilliant was Lillian Board?' It was 'How did she lose?'"
  - That exact reframe fits Kejelcha perfectly.
- **Redemption beat:** a rematch against the woman who beat her ("this time she refused to let history repeat itself").
- **Chapter turns are one-liners:** "She just couldn't see it." / "No one knew she would never get the chance." Each chapter ends on a forward-pointing sentence, which is a micro open loop.
- **What we take:** reveal the ending early and make the "why/how" the loop, the "how did he lose?" reframe, and chapter-ending lines that point forward.

### Video 3: "Chasing Sub 2" (adidas, 10.6M, 12.5 min)

- **Hook:** coach Berardelli in close-up, quiet: "I asked Sabastian not to fear. To believe that he might be the one to do the Sub2." This is a **whispered thesis from a mentor**, not a narrator.
- **No voiceover at all.** The whole film is interviews (coach, brand staff, athlete, Haile's agent) cut over slow cinematic B-roll: Kenyan dirt roads at dawn, shoe macro shots, lab treadmills.
- **Structure:** belief → history (Haile 2008, "sub-2:05 bonus… the agent laughed") → the athlete (farmer, father of three) → the team → the tech → **race-call finale**.
  - The last 90 s is live commentary over the race: "He's got just under a minute… Come on, Sabastian… Under two hours. Nobody has ever done this… 1:59:30."
  - Then **20 s of silence and music** before one closing line: "Humanity just got faster."
- **Visual grammar (the Netflix/Apple look the user wants):**
  - shallow depth-of-field interviews, off-centre framing, soft key light
  - macro product shots
  - slow push-ins
  - lower-thirds that are just a name and a role in thin sans
  - the time "01:59:30" as a clean centred number, no slam
- **What we take:** let the commentary carry the climax, hold the silence after the finish, and use a mentor quote as the opener. Haile Gebrselassie is Kejelcha's mentor ("Haile always tell me: you are make a history one day"), which mirrors adidas' structure.

### Video 4: "The Real Reason The Sub 2 Hour Marathon Barrier Was Finally Broken" (Adam Jansen, 185K, 18x)

- **Hook:** "the last remaining great barrier… Sprinters had sub-10. Milers had sub-4. Endurance runners now have sub-2." That is **a ladder of barriers** in 15 s.
- **Structure:** history of attempts (Breaking2 at Monza 2:00:25 → INEOS Vienna 1:59:40 → Kiptum) → setup of the London field, where **Kejelcha is introduced as the "dark horse"** → split-by-split race walk-through → "is there an explanation?" (shoes).
- **Race told through splits:** "5 km 14:14… half 1:00:29… the last two 5 km splits 13:54 and 13:42". The gap grows as he narrates it.
  - That maps directly onto a **split ticker / gap ticker** graphic.
- **Key line, unprompted:** "truly the Ethiopian is the best runner to never win a gold medal." **The audience already feels this.** (It's factually loose, see section 0.)
- **What we take:** the barrier-ladder context, splits as tension, and an explicit open question at mid-point ("but why?").

### Video 5: "There May Never Be Another GOAT" (Lane 6, 115K on 1.6K subs)

- **Hook:** "One simple fact can prove it to you… When was the last time someone came within a 10th of a second of Bolt? No one has." **A question to the viewer in the first 15 s**, answered with one stat.
- **Pattern break at 2:42:** "Here's the graph I originally showed you, but with a twist. **Can you guess what the red highlights mean?**" The same graphic is re-shown with new information. That is a strong retention device.
- **What we take:** an interactive question in the first 15 s, and **re-using one graphic with a twist** later.

### Concrete techniques to use (modern, cinematic, no stickers)

- **Race-clock overlay:** a clean, thin, white monospaced clock locked to the screen corner during race footage. It ticks in real time and freezes on the finish, with no slam. At 1:59:30 it splits into two rows: SAWE 1:59:30 / KEJELCHA 1:59:41.
- **Gap ticker:** a small "GAP +0:04 → +0:07 → +0:11" readout that updates over the last 2 km (London) and the last 100 m (Tokyo, "+0.06").
- **Split-screen time comparison:** the 4 near-misses side by side as a 2x2 grid of fan-shot clips, each freezing at the line with its margin under it: 0.01 / 0.88 / 0.06 / 11.
- **Map zoom transition:** a satellite map of Ethiopia (Addis / Oromia) pulls up and out to London and dives into The Mall finish. It is a clean 3D-camera move, not a cartoon.
- **Motion-tracked labels:** a thin line + name tag tracked to Kejelcha in the pack (since he is the one people overlook), e.g. "KEJELCHA · DEBUT".
- **3D parallax photos:** for the Millrose 2019 and Paris 2024 stills, where no usable video exists.
- **Speed ramp into slow-mo** on every "line moment" (Gressier passing, Sawe pulling away at 41 km). Ramp back to real time on the clock freeze.
- **Hard cut to silence** after each loss. The ambient crowd keeps going while music drops out for 1-1.5 s. Do this 3-4 times max.
- **Typography:** one modern condensed sans (e.g. Inter Tight / Söhne-like, or Bebas only for the title cards), white at 90% opacity on a darkened frame. Words fade/track in over 12-18 frames, with no bounce or scale-slam.
- **Chapter cards** are just the margin: "0.01", "0.06", "11 SECONDS", in large thin type on black for 1.5 s, with a low hit.

## 6. Three video concepts

### Concept A: "11 Seconds" (race-as-spine, cold open on the finish). **Recommended.**

- **Cold open (0:00-0:25):**
  - Black screen, then the fan-shot London clip: Sawe turning onto The Mall.
  - Race-clock overlay reads 1:59:2x.
  - Commentary: "Under two hours. Nobody has ever done this…"
  - The crowd erupts and the clock freezes at **1:59:30**.
  - **Hard cut to silence.**
  - Camera holds on the empty road behind Sawe. 11 s of the gap ticker counting up.
  - Kejelcha appears. Clock 1:59:41.
  - VO (user): "Dat's da 2nd fastest marathon ever run. In his first one. And he lost."
- **Structure:**
  - The London race is the spine, cut into 4 segments: start / halfway / 30 km Sawe move / last mile.
  - Between segments, a **flashback chapter** that matches the race moment:
    - **30 km:** the Sawe move ↔ the Millrose mile, 0.01 off the WR, then he breaks it 3 weeks later.
    - **Last mile, he drops back:** ↔ Tokyo 2025, leads into the straight, Gressier gets him by 0.06.
    - **The final straight:** ↔ Paris 2024, ran for the team, 6th.
  - Finish: London 1:59:41 → his own words: "After 41 km my leg is done… even I don't believe myself."
- **Payoff / closer:** Haile's line ("you are make a history one day"), then the open question: is he a choke artist, or just born in the wrong era, the Sawe/Cheptegei era?

### Concept B: "0.01 → 0.06 → 11" (the near-miss ladder, Powell-style chronology)

- **Cold open:** a 2x2 split-screen of four finish lines, each freezing on its margin (0.01, 0.88, 0.06, 11 s). One cream word at a time: "Close." "Closer." "Closest." "Fastest." Then cut to black.
- **Structure:**
  - Chronological chapters, each titled with its margin.
  - Each chapter repeats the Powell loop on purpose: big build-up → near-miss → "…and weeks later he does something crazy" (Boston WR after Millrose, the half-marathon WR, the sub-2 debut).
  - Mid-point twist (Lane 6 trick): re-show the 2x2 grid with a fifth box added, London.
- **Closer:** "Everybody remembers who ran 1:59:30. Who remembers 1:59:41?"

### Concept C: "The Man in the Background" (perspective piece, adidas-style interview film)

- **Cold open:** Sawe's winner's press-conference soundbite crediting Kejelcha (AP Archive / CITIUS). Hold on Kejelcha's face, then the title: the man in the background of every great race.
- **Structure:**
  - Each chapter re-watches a famous race where Kejelcha is **not** the story: Cheptegei's Doha gold, Gressier's Tokyo shock, Sawe's WR. A motion-tracked label finds him in the frame every time.
  - Interviews carry it: his CITIUS / LetsRun quotes, Haile, coaches. Minimal VO.
- **Closer:** "Next time y'all watch a world record, look who's right behind it."

**Best concept: A.** It opens on the single strongest real moment (the 11-second silence), keeps the London race as a tension spine viewers already half-know, and uses the near-misses as flashbacks. That cures the mid-video retention dip. It also avoids the "Lemme tell y'all → chapters → what y'all think" format, as CLAUDE.md asks.
