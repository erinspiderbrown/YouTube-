August 5, 2017. London. World Championships, 100 meters.

## 2. Who he was
Justin Gatlin from Brooklyn, raised in Pensacola, Florida.

When he was 9 years old, doctors diagnosed him with attention deficit disorder. ADD. And from then on, he took his medicine every day. Just like a lot of kids do.

He get to the University of Tennessee, and he a freshman running crazy times. He win NCAA titles his very 1st year.

Everybody can see it. This kid gon' be the next big thing in American sprinting.

## 3. Strike 1
June 2001. The USATF Junior Championships.

Now his medicine at the time was Adderall. And Adderall got amphetamine in it. Amphetamine is on the banned list.

So what Justin do? He stop taking his medicine a few days before the meet. Around June 13. He thinking it'll be out his system by race day.

It wasn't.

He test positive for amphetamine.

No steroids. No needles. The same pill he been taking since he was 9. He just ain't have the paperwork filed to say he was allowed to take it.

Now listen to what the arbitration panel said. Not me. The panel.

They said Gatlin "neither cheated nor did he intend to cheat."

They said he "certainly is not a doper."

They called it "at most, a technical or a paperwork violation."

[SOUNDBITE: Gatlin interview about the ADHD case]

They still banned him. 2 years. Then they cut it, and in July 2002 he was back.

But here go the catch. It still counted. On his record, that's strike 1. And they told him straight up: 1 more, and that's a lifetime ban.

## 4. The rise
And mane… he came back like nothing happened.

2004. Athens Olympics. Justin Gatlin wins the 100 meters. 9.85. Olympic champion at 22.

2005. World Championships. He wins the 100 and the 200. The double.

May 2006. Doha. He runs 9.77. Ties the world record.

[SOUNDBITE: Doha 2006, the 9.77]

He the face of American sprinting. The fastest man in the world.

For about 2 months.

## 5. Strike 2
'Cause in July 2006, the news drops.

Back in April, April 22, 2006, at the Kansas Relays, he tested positive. This time for testosterone.

Now this one real. I ain't gon' sit here and act like it ain't.

His coach, Trevor Graham, said a massage therapist rubbed a testosterone cream on him without him knowing. The massage therapist denied it. Gatlin said he "never knowingly used any banned substance."

But here go what nobody talk about. Remember strike 1? The ADHD medicine?

That's why he was looking at a lifetime ban. Not 2 years. Life.

[SOUNDBITE: 2006 news report on the ban]

He ended up taking 8 years. Part of that was 'cause he helped federal investigators. And part of it was what they called the "exceptional circumstances" of that 1st test.

Then in December 2007, a panel cut it to 4 years.

He fought it all the way up. In 2008 he even took it to federal court, saying they was punishing him for his disability. And the judge said he believed Gatlin "is being wronged."

But he said he couldn't do nothing about it.

So no Beijing 2008. They erased his 9.77. And Justin Gatlin, the Olympic champion, sat out 4 years in the middle of his prime.

## 6. The comeback
2010. He comes back in a little meet in Estonia. Runs 10.24.

Everybody figured he was done.

2012. London Olympics. Bronze. 9.79.

2015. He runs 9.74. At 33 years old. That's his personal best.

And then the World Championships. He lose to Usain Bolt… by 1 hundredth of a second.

2016. Rio. Silver. Again behind Bolt.

And every time he step on the line, they booing.

## 7. The villain
'Cause by now the story was set. Bolt the hero. Gatlin the villain.

So back to London. 2017. Gatlin wins gold. Beats Bolt in his last 100.

And the crowd boos him before the race, after the race, and during the medal ceremony.

Seb Coe, the head of world track, said Gatlin "should have been banned for life."

[SOUNDBITE: Bolt on the booing]

But you know who defended him? Usain Bolt. Bolt said the booing was unfair. He said Gatlin "had done his time."

Then later that year a newspaper went undercover, and they filmed Gatlin's coach and his agent allegedly offering to get people drugs.

Gatlin fired his coach. And when they investigated, the agent had no evidence Gatlin was doping. No new failed test. Nothing.

He kept running. 2019, at 37 years old, he still got a silver at the World Championships and a gold in the relay.

Then in 2022, on his 40th birthday, he retired.

## 8. So is he really a super doper?
Now I'ma keep it real with y'all.

Justin Gatlin got 2 strikes. The 2006 test was real, and he did 4 years for it.

But that 1st strike? That was a kid taking the medicine his doctor gave him since he was 9. The panel said it themselves. Not a cheater. Not a doper. Paperwork.

And today? In 2017, a college tennis player tested positive for his ADHD medicine. Same thing. They gave him an exemption after the fact and cleared him.

If Gatlin got that in 2001, there ain't no strike 1. 2006 would've been his 1st offense. And we probably talking about him different today.

So when people call him a 2-time doper and leave out the ADHD part… they telling you half the story.
