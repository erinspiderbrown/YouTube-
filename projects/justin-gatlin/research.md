# Justin Gatlin: "the super doper" (research, 11 Nov 2026)

The user's angle: Gatlin failed 2 tests, but the first was his ADHD medicine, and people leave that out and call him a serial doper.
Keep it honest: strike 1 was a technical/paperwork case (the panel's own words); strike 2 (2006 testosterone) is real, and he served 4 years.

## Verified facts
- **Diagnosis and medicine:** diagnosed with ADD at age 9 and took prescribed medication ever since; for the five years before 2001 it was Adderall, which contains a banned amphetamine. (3wire Sports, quoting the 2001–02 arbitration record)
- **Strike 1:**
  - **The test:** USATF Junior National Championships, June 16–17, 2001 (USADA testing, per the CAS filing). Positive for amphetamine. He had stopped the medicine around June 13: the panel said the declining sample amounts were "consistent with … having stopped taking his medication on or about June 13."
  - **The panel's words:** Gatlin "neither cheated nor did he intend to cheat", he "certainly is not a doper", and it was "at most, a 'technical' or a 'paperwork' violation."
  - **The sanction:** a 2-year IAAF ban, reduced (sources say to 1 year; 3wire says a 14-month suspension). The IAAF granted early reinstatement in July 2002.
  - **The catch:** it still counted as strike 1. The IAAF warned him that another violation would mean a lifetime ban.
- **College:** Tennessee. He won NCAA titles as a freshman in 2001.
- **The rise:**
  - Athens 2004: Olympic 100m gold, 9.85.
  - 2005 World Championships (Helsinki): 100m gold (9.88) and 200m gold.
  - Doha, May 2006: 9.77 (first shown as 9.76) to tie the world record. It was later annulled.
- **Strike 2:**
  - **The test:** a relay race in Lawrence, Kansas (Kansas Relays) on April 22, 2006. Positive for testosterone "or its precursor"; the B sample was confirmed in July.
  - **The explanation:** his coach Trevor Graham blamed massage therapist Christopher Whetstine, saying he rubbed a testosterone cream on Gatlin without his knowledge. Whetstine denied it.
  - **Gatlin's words:** he said "I cannot account for these results" and that he had "never knowingly used any banned substance."
  - **Background:** Graham had eight athletes who tested positive or were banned.
- **The punishment:**
  - Because 2001 counted, he faced life. On August 22, 2006 he accepted 8 years (avoiding life) because he cooperated with federal investigators and because of the "exceptional circumstances" of the first test.
  - In December 2007 an arbitration panel cut it to 4 years. USADA called that "a fair and just outcome."
  - In June 2008 CAS upheld the 4 years, unanimously, and rejected erasing the 2001 case.
  - June 2008, his federal lawsuit under the Americans with Disabilities Act: Judge Lacey Collier (Pensacola) said he believed Gatlin "is being wronged" but had no jurisdiction. He missed Beijing 2008.
- **The comeback:**
  - August 3, 2010: back in Rakvere, Estonia, winning in 10.24.
  - 2012: London Olympic 100m bronze (9.79) and World Indoor 60m gold (6.46).
  - 2013: World Championships 100m silver (9.85; Bolt 9.77).
  - 2015: personal best 9.74 in Doha at age 33. World Championships silver in both the 100m (0.01 behind Bolt) and the 200m (19.74).
  - 2016: Rio Olympic 100m silver (9.89; Bolt 9.81).
- **London 2017:**
  - **The race:** World Championships 100m gold in 9.92 (Coleman 9.94, Bolt 9.95, Bolt's last individual race).
  - **The reaction:** he was booed before and after the race and at the medal ceremony. Seb Coe said he "should have been banned for life." Bolt called the booing unfair and said Gatlin "had done his time." Gatlin's father called the booing "disrespectful to the sport."
- **The 2017 Telegraph sting:** an undercover reporter, posing as a film producer, filmed coach Dennis Mitchell and agent Robert Wagner allegedly offering to supply HGH and testosterone. Gatlin fired Mitchell and said he was innocent. The AIU said Wagner had no information or evidence to back his claims that Gatlin was doping or that Mitchell was involved. Wagner served a 2-year ban. No new violation was ever found against Gatlin.
- **2019:** World Championships 100m silver (9.89) and 4x100m gold, at 37.
- **Retirement:** announced in 2022, on his 40th birthday.
- **ADHD today:** in 2017 a Duke tennis player (Spencer Furman) tested positive for amphetamine from his prescribed ADHD medicine (Vyvanse) and was cleared after a retroactive exemption. Retroactive exemptions are mostly for athletes below the elite level.
