# What Happened To The Last 10 NCAA 100m Champions? (script v1, ~1,550 words ≈ 10:30 at the NCAA pace)

Angle: an NCAA title guarantees nothing. Chronological, 2016 → 2026 (2020 cancelled).
Burrell: respectful tribute, no cause of death, resource line on screen.

---

## 01 · Cold open
Ten men. Ten national titles. Ten "fastest man in college" crowns.

One became world champion. One is banned until 2029. One's still trying to prove a steak didn't end his career. And one of 'em? Isn't here anymore.

Every June, somebody wins the NCAA 100 and the whole sport says the same thing: this is the next one. So I went back and checked. Every men's champion since 2016. Where they are now.

And the answer is gonna tell you exactly how much an NCAA title is really worth.

## 02 · 2016 · Jarrion Lawson, Arkansas
2016. Jarrion Lawson does something nobody had done since Jesse Owens in 1936. He wins the 100, the 200, *and* the long jump at the same NCAA championships.

A year later he's got a world silver medal in the long jump. He's 23. The sky's the limit.

Then 2018. He tests positive for trenbolone, an anabolic steroid. Lawson says it came from beef he ate at a restaurant the day before the test. Nobody believes him. In 2019 he gets four years.

But here's the thing. He fought it. And in 2020, the Court of Arbitration for Sport ruled he bore *no fault or negligence*. The meat was more likely than not contaminated. Ban gone.

Problem is, the clean verdict doesn't give you back the two years. He missed the 2019 Worlds. Then a knee injury. He's still out here jumping. He went 8.14 in Zagreb this summer. But the guy who matched Jesse Owens? The world never really got to see what he would've been.

## 03 · 2017 · Christian Coleman, Tennessee
2017. Christian Coleman runs 9.82 in college. That's still the collegiate record. Nobody's touched it in nine years.

He turns pro, and two years later he's world champion. 9.76 in Doha. This is the one the hype was right about.

But even the success story got messy. Missed drug tests. Not failed, *missed*. Whereabouts violations. He gets banned and misses the Tokyo Olympics in his prime.

He came back. World indoor 60 gold in 2024. World relay gold. Diamond League titles. And then in February of this year, he's arrested alongside Sha'Carri Richardson. He pleaded not guilty.

So yeah, Coleman made it. The best NCAA sprinter of the decade made it. And it still came with a ban and an arrest.

## 04 · 2018 · Cameron Burrell, Houston
2018. Cameron Burrell wins it for Houston. And if anybody was born for this, it was him.

His dad, Leroy Burrell, held the 100 world record and coached him at Houston. His mom, Michelle Finn-Burrell, is an Olympic gold medalist too. His godfather? Carl Lewis.

He won the 100 and anchored Houston's 4x1 to another national title. That was his last college season.

In August 2021, Cameron Burrell passed away. He was 26 years old.

There's no stat line for this one. Just a young man who carried a famous name, ran with it, and was loved by a lot of people. Rest easy, Cam.

## 05 · 2019 · Divine Oduduru, Texas Tech
2019. Divine Oduduru, Nigeria, Texas Tech. He runs 9.86 *and* 19.73 at the same meet. That's a double almost nobody in the world could touch.

Then the feds get involved. A therapist named Eric Lira becomes the first person ever charged under the Rodchenkov Act, the American law that makes doping conspiracy a crime. In the court papers, there's "Athlete 1" — that's Blessing Okagbare. And "Athlete 2."

Athlete 2 was Divine Oduduru.

He's banned six years, until 2029. Everything he ran after July 2021? Erased. He still says he's innocent, that he got set up. But his career, at least on paper, is over.

## 06 · 2021 · Terrance Laird, LSU
2020 got cancelled by COVID. So the next champ is 2021, Terrance Laird, LSU.

And this dude was *special*. He wins the 100 and the 200 at nationals. Turns pro. Signs with Adidas.

But here's what nobody knew. He'd been racing that whole season on a back that was falling apart. Spinal decompression, twice a week, just to get to the line.

Then the discs go. Herniated. He misses all of 2022. He fights back in 2023 and wins a 200 in 20.06. People thought he was back.

And then, quiet. Since then he's barely raced at all. Just a guy the forums keep asking about: "Whatever happened to Terrance Laird?" The body just wouldn't let him be who he was.

## 07 · 2022 · Joseph Fahnbulleh, Florida
2022. Joseph Fahnbulleh, Florida, running for Liberia.

This one's different, 'cause he's still in it. Two Olympic 200 finals, Tokyo and Paris. African champion in the 100 *and* 200. He owns the Liberian record at 19.83. He's still lining up in the Diamond League.

But be real. No global medal yet. And that's the part nobody tells you. For a lot of NCAA champs, "making it" just means grinding on the circuit, one paycheck at a time.

## 08 · 2023 · Courtney Lindsey, Texas Tech
2023. Courtney Lindsey, Texas Tech again. Signs with Nike.

And on paper he's legit. 19.71 in the 200. 9.82 in the 100. That's fast. That's world-class fast.

But this is America. At the 2026 USA Championships, he runs 9.97 and finishes fifth. Fifth. In the country with the deepest sprint pool on earth, 9.8 gets you a nice Instagram post and a plane ticket home.

## 09 · 2024 · Louie Hinchliffe, Houston
2024. Louie Hinchliffe. A British kid who joins Carl Lewis's squad at Houston and turns into a 9.95 NCAA champion. Weeks later, he's in the *Olympic* 100 final in Paris.

That's a fairy tale. So he turns pro. Signs with ASICS. Leaves Houston.

And 2025 is a nightmare. A stress fracture. Hamstring problems. And he loses his father.

So he does something almost nobody does. He gives up the pro contract, goes through the NCAA reinstatement process, and goes *back* to college. Back to Houston. Back to Carl Lewis.

Think about that. An Olympic finalist decided the safest place for his career was a college track.

## 10 · 2025 · Jordan Anthony, Arkansas
2025. Jordan Anthony. And he wasn't even a track guy. He was a *football* player. A wide receiver who'd bounced from Kentucky to Texas A&M to Arkansas.

He wins the NCAA 100, walks away from football, and signs with Adidas. Then he wins the Bowerman, the Heisman of college track. First football player ever to do it.

And in March of this year? World indoor 60 champion.

The guy who barely got targets on Saturdays is a world champion. Nobody saw that coming.

## 11 · 2026 · Kanyinsola Ajayi, Auburn
And that brings us to right now. 2026. Kanyinsola Ajayi, Auburn, Nigeria.

He runs 9.84 at regionals. Number two in NCAA history, behind only Coleman. Wins nationals in a wind-aided 9.72. Then he goes and beats the pros. Wins the Prefontaine Classic. Wins the London Diamond League. Commonwealth bronze.

He's 22. He's got the hype. He's got the times.

But so did every name in this video.

## 12 · The verdict
So let's add it up. Ten champions.

One world 100 meter champion: Coleman. One more world title, but indoors and over 60: Anthony. One Olympic final: Hinchliffe.

On the other side: one banned until 2029. One who lost two years to a steak and never got 'em back. One whose back gave out. One who went back to college. And one who's gone way too soon.

An NCAA title doesn't make you. It just means for one day in June, you were the fastest guy in college. What happens after that? Injuries, testers, agents, money, luck. None of that's on the results sheet.

So the next time somebody wins the NCAA 100 and the whole internet says "he's the next one"…

Run it back. Ask these nine. And remember the tenth.
