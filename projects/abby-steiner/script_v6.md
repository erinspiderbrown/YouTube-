## 1. Cold open
Summer 2022. Abby Steiner is the fastest woman in America in the 200. A 2-time world champion. 22 years old.

[SOUNDBITE: the 21.77 race call]

Summer 2024. She crying in front of the cameras at the Olympic Trials.

[SOUNDBITE: Abby crying after the Trials]

And after that?

She just… disappeared.

No races. No Olympics. Nothing.

So what happened to Abby Steiner? Lemme tell y'all.

## 2. The come up
Abby Steiner from Dublin, Ohio. Dublin Coffman High School.

And she wasn't just good in high school. She was one of the fastest girls in the whole country.

4 straight state titles in the 200. State records in the 100 and the 200. Top 10 in the nation. And she was a soccer star too. All-state forward.

She even tore her ACL in high school. Missed most of a year. Came back anyway.

And now let's keep it real. In sprinting, the top of the women's 200 is mostly Black women. So a white girl from Ohio running with the best in the country? People noticed.

Then she went to Kentucky. And in college she took off.

## 3. The peak
June 2022. The NCAA final. 21.80.

[SOUNDBITE: the 21.80 race call]

The fastest a college woman has ever run the 200. Ever.

2 weeks later, US Championships. 21.77. US champion.

Then the World Championships. 2 gold medals. The 4x100 and the 4x400.

[SOUNDBITE: Abby after the relay gold]

The Bowerman award. A pro deal with PUMA. Reportedly 2 million dollars.

She had everything. And she was just getting started.

## 4. The first crack
2023 started fine. She broke the American record indoors in the 300.

But then… something wasn't right. Her heels.

At USAs she finish 4th in the 200. Top 3 go to Worlds. She ain't make the team.

And then her season got shut down early.

## 5. Both feet
What she had is called Haglund's. That's a bone bump on the back of your heel that rubs on the Achilles. Every single step, it's grinding.

And she had it in both feet.

August 2023, surgery on the left foot. End of September, surgery on the right.

Now here go the problem. The Olympic Trials is June 2024.

She said the 1st time she even jogged on a track again was around the beginning of December.

6 months. That's all she had. To go from 2 foot surgeries to trying to make the Olympic team. In the deepest sprint country in the world.

## 6. The last race
2024. Olympic year.

She did get back. She was running fast again.

But right before the Trials, her foot started acting up again. And she said they "thought it was best to train through" it.

Olympic Trials. 200 meter final.

6th. No Paris.

[SOUNDBITE: Abby at the Trials, "no one comes here wanting to do anything other than represent the country in the Olympics"]

That was the last time we saw Abby Steiner race.

## 7. The disappearance
After the Trials, the pain came back in her left foot.

Another surgery. That's at least 3 surgeries in 2 years.

2025 came. She didn't run 1 race. Not 1.

No meets. No updates. Just gone.

Then August 2025, she finally posted. She said she was "taking a step back from running for the time being to give myself the time I need to get healthy."

And that was it.

Today she in grad school at the University of South Carolina. Master's in exercise science.

She even blames her old spikes for her injuries. She took her shoe company to court over it. But that's a whole other video.

The fastest college woman ever… hasn't raced in over 2 years.

## 8. What went wrong
So what really went wrong?

Number 1. 2022 never ended. She raced from January indoors all the way to the end of July at Worlds. At Worlds somebody asked her if she was finally done. She said, "We'll be done after this meet."

Number 2. Her heels. Haglund's in both feet. 2 surgeries in 2 months.

Number 3. The clock. 6 months from jogging again to the Olympic Trials.

Number 4. She trained through the pain. And the pain won.

Her 21.77 would've medaled in the Paris Olympic final. She was 22. Sprinters peak around 26, 27.

She should be in her prime right now.

Now I'ma keep it real with y'all.

How often you see a white girl from Ohio running with the fastest women on the planet? And beating 'em?

Abby did that. For 1 summer, she was as fast as anybody in the world.

Then her body gave out. And she disappeared.

So what y'all think? Was it the season? The surgeries? Did she rush back?

And if she stayed healthy… would Abby Steiner be an Olympic champion right now?
