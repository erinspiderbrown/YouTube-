## 1. Cold open
June 2022. Hayward Field.

A white girl from Ohio lines up in the 200… against the fastest women in America.

And she beat all of 'em.

[SOUNDBITE: the 21.77 race call]

21.77. US champion. At that moment, the fastest time in the whole world that year.

4 weeks later she a 2-time world champion.

Then she just… disappeared.

No races. No Olympics. Nothing.

Her name is Abby Steiner. And where she went is crazier than y'all think.

## 2. Why she was different
Now let's just keep it real.

In sprinting today, the top of the women's 200 is mostly Black women. America. Jamaica. The Caribbean. That's just facts. Go look at any Olympic final.

So when a white girl from Dublin, Ohio starts running with them… and beating them… people noticed.

Y'all remember Matthew Boling. "White Lightning." Same thing. The hype was different.

And Abby? Abby actually backed it up.

## 3. The soccer girl
And here go the crazy part. Track wasn't even her first sport.

She was a soccer player. All-state forward.

She ain't start running track till 8th grade. Then in high school she tore her ACL and missed most of a year.

Came back anyway. 14 state titles.

Kentucky gave her a scholarship for both. Soccer and track. Her freshman year she started 19 soccer games.

Then she made a choice. No more soccer. Just track.

## 4. The run
2021. She win the NCAA indoor title in the 200.

2022. She run 22.09 indoors. The American record.

Then June 11, 2022. The NCAA outdoor final. 21.80.

[SOUNDBITE: the 21.80 race call]

The fastest a college woman has ever run the 200. Ever.

2 weeks later, same track. US Championships. 21.77. She beat everybody.

Then the World Championships. Same track again.

5th in the 200. But in the relays? Gold in the 4x100. Gold in the 4x400.

[SOUNDBITE: relay race call]

Then they gave her the Bowerman. The biggest award in college track.

She 22 years old. 2-time world champion. And PUMA signs her. Reportedly a 2 million dollar deal.

Everybody thought she was the next big thing.

## 5. The disappearance
But in 2023, something wasn't right.

Her heels.

She still made it to USAs. 4th in the 200.

But she ain't make it to Worlds in Budapest. She was hurt.

Then the surgery. Both feet. They took bone out of her heels… and reattached her Achilles.

[SOUNDBITE: Abby on the surgery]

2024. Olympic year. She fight her way back to the Trials. Makes the 200 final.

6th. 22.24. No Paris.

And that was it. That was her last race.

The pain came back in her left foot. Another surgery. At least 3 surgeries total.

2025, she didn't run 1 race.

August 2025, she posted it herself. She said she was "taking a step back from running for the time being to give myself the time I need to get healthy."

She went back to school. Master's degree. Exercise science.

The fastest college woman ever… and she just gone.

## 6. Where she went
Then April 24, 2026. Abby Steiner shows back up.

But not on the track.

In court.

She sues PUMA. And the Mercedes Formula 1 team.

Yeah. The F1 team. The lawsuit says they helped design the spikes.

She names the shoes. The Deviate Nitro Elite 2. The Deviate Nitro Elite 3. And the evoSPEED Tokyo Nitro.

Her lawsuit says the carbon plate and the foam "changed the foot and ankle mechanics during running." It says they caused bone stress injuries. It calls the shoes "defective" and "unsafe."

She asking for more than 1.25 million dollars. Medical bills. Lost earnings. And "the loss of full enjoyment of life."

## 7. Then PUMA hit back
But it ain't stop there.

June 2026. 2 more PUMA athletes sue PUMA. American 400 runner Champion Allison. And Jamaican Olympian Damion Thomas. Same lawyer. Same claims. Their feet.

PUMA's answer? They said: "We strongly deny any allegation that our performance products cause injuries."

They asked the judge in Massachusetts to throw Abby's case out. Or move it to private arbitration.

Then, according to her lawyers… PUMA went to Germany.

July 2, 2026. A court in Nuremberg. PUMA files a case against all 3 of 'em. 11.6 million euros. That's about 13 million dollars.

Her lawyers say PUMA wants to take away their right to sue… and make them pay PUMA's legal bills.

Think about that. She sued for 1.25 million. PUMA came back for 10 times that.

Her lawyers said PUMA is using a German court "to punish and intimidate U.S. athletes."

PUMA ain't said that publicly. And none of it's been decided. But that's where it's at right now.
