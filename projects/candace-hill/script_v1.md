## 1. Cold open
June 20, 2015. Shoreline, Washington.

A 16-year-old girl from Georgia lines up in the 100.

And runs 10.98.

[SOUNDBITE: the 10.98 race and the reaction]

10.98. At 16 years old. A high school sophomore.

No high school girl had ever broken 11 seconds. Ever.

Her name was Candace Hill.

And 6 months later, she did somethin' no American track athlete had ever done at her age. She skipped college… and went pro.

So what happened?

## 2. The fastest girl alive
Candace from Conyers, Georgia. Rockdale County High School.

And that 10.98 wasn't no fluke.

That same summer she goes to the World Youth Championships in Cali, Colombia, and she wins the 100… and the 200. 22.43 in the 200. To this day, that's still her best.

She wins Gatorade National Athlete of the Year. As a sophomore. You know the only other sophomore to ever win that?

Marion Jones.

They was callin' her the fastest girl alive. Everybody wanted her. USC. Florida. Every big program in the country.

## 3. The deal
December 17, 2015.

Candace Hill signs a 10-year professional contract with ASICS.

She 16 years old. The youngest American track athlete to ever go pro.

[SOUNDBITE: news report on the signing]

Now think about what that means. No college track. No NCAA. You go pro, you can't run for a school no more.

ASICS ain't say how much it was worth. They said it would cover her college tuition. And it was built to last all the way through her prime. Until she 26.

At the time? It looked like the smartest move in track. Get paid now. Don't wait.

## 4. The pressure
2016. The Olympic year. And Candace is 17.

She miss about 4 months of training that year.

She still makes it to the Olympic Trials. 13th in the 100. 14th in the 200.

Then she goes to the World Under-20 Championships and wins gold. 100 meters, 11.07. And another gold in the relay.

So the talent was still there.

But then… it just stops.

2017. 2018. 2019. Not 1 personal best. In 2019 at USAs, she finish 14th in the 100 and 14th in the 200.

And while that's happening, she going through coaches. By 2023, she been through 5 different coaching changes.

## 5. "You made the wrong decision"
And here go the part nobody saw.

In 2021, she opened up about it to World Athletics.

She said: "I had so many people constantly in my ear saying, you made the wrong decision." And she said, "It had me second-guessing myself."

[SOUNDBITE: Candace Hill interview]

Mane, imagine that. You 16. You sign a deal. And for the next few years, every time you lose, the whole internet telling you that you messed up your life.

She started seeing a therapist. And she said her therapist told her that some of your emotional issues can turn into physical pain.

She said, "If your mind is not in a great space, your body quickly follows suit."

## 6. The comeback
But Candace ain't quit.

2021. She runs 10.99. 1 hundredth off the time she ran at 16. And she makes the Olympic Trials final. 8th.

Then she move to Baton Rouge to train under Dennis Shaver, the longtime LSU coach, with pros like Aleia Hobbs. And at the same time, she back in school. Studying pre-med.

2024. She runs the fastest 150 of the whole year.

Then the Olympic Trials. She makes the 100 meter final again.

[SOUNDBITE: 2024 Trials 100m final]

7th. 11.00.

Sha'Carri won that race. Candace was in it.

## 7. Where she at now
2025, she still out there racing.

But here go the crazy part.

Her best 100 time ever… is still the 10.98 she ran at 16 years old.

The girl who was supposed to be the next big thing in American sprinting… never made an Olympic team.

## 8. So was going pro a mistake?
Now I'ma keep it real with y'all.

Candace Hill ain't fail. She made 2 Olympic Trials finals. She won world youth and world junior titles as a teenager. She went back to school.

But the question still the same.

Do you take the money at 16? Or do you go to college, run NCAA, and grow up first?

'Cause a lot of the people who said she made the wrong decision… they wasn't the ones being offered that check.

So what y'all think? Was going pro at 16 a mistake?

And if you was in her shoes, would you have signed?
