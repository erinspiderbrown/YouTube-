## 1. Cold open
May 27, 2001. Eugene, Oregon. The Prefontaine Classic.

An 18-year-old high school senior lines up in the mile… against grown men. Pros.

[SOUNDBITE: the 3:53.43 race call]

3:53.43.

That's the fastest mile a high schooler has ever run. And 25 years later? Nobody has touched it.

They said he was gonna save American miling.

He never won a single Olympic or world medal.

So what happened to Alan Webb? Lemme tell y'all.

## 2. The kid from Reston
Alan Webb. South Lakes High School. Reston, Virginia.

As a sophomore he ran 4:06 in the mile. That broke Jim Ryun's national sophomore record.

His senior year, January 2001, New York. He runs 3:59.86 indoors. First American high schooler ever under 4 minutes indoors.

Then May. Pre Classic. And he breaks Jim Ryun's high school record. A record that stood for 36 years.

He was on David Letterman. Track & Field News made him the high school athlete of the year.

## 3. The savior
Now y'all gotta understand what was going on in the mile back then.

Americans wasn't winning it. The world record belonged to Hicham El Guerrouj from Morocco. 3:43.13. The best milers on the planet were from Morocco, Kenya, Algeria.

And here come a white kid from the suburbs of D.C. running 3:53 in high school.

People wasn't just calling him fast. They was calling him the future of American distance running.

## 4. Michigan
So he goes to Michigan.

Big Ten champion in cross country. All-American. Big Ten champion in the 1500.

And then 1 year in… he leaves.

He goes back to his high school coach, Scott Raczko. And he signs with Nike.

19 years old. A pro.

## 5. Athens
2004. Olympic year.

He wins the Pre Classic mile in 3:50. Then he wins the Olympic Trials.

[SOUNDBITE: Webb wins the 2004 Bowerman Mile]

So he goes to Athens. America's great hope in the 1500.

First round. 9th in his heat. Out.

He never even made the final.

## 6. The year everything clicked
And then he came back. And he got better.

2005, he made the World Championships final. 9th.

But 2007? 2007 was crazy.

3:30 in the 1500. 1:43 in the 800.

And in July, in Belgium, he runs the mile in 3:46.91. The American record.

That record stood for 16 years.

He wins USAs. He goes to the World Championships in Osaka as one of the fastest milers on the planet.

8th.

And the gold? It went to an American. Just not him. Bernard Lagat.

## 7. The fall
2008. Olympic Trials. He needs top 3.

5th. No Beijing.

2009, he moves to Portland to train with Alberto Salazar.

2010, Achilles surgery.

2011, he leaves Salazar.

He moves up to the 5000 and the 10,000. Nothing.

February 2014. He runs one last mile at Millrose. And he retires. 31 years old.

Then he tries triathlon.

His first race? He crashes on the bike.

## 8. What went wrong
So what really went wrong?

Number 1. The hype. He was the next Jim Ryun at 18. Every race after that, people compared him to a kid in high school.

Number 2. Championships. Webb could run fast. In rabbit races, in Europe, he was flying. But the big championship races with no pacers? That's where it never came together.

Number 3. The training. He said it himself. He went too hard in the weight room. He said he went "full Arnold Schwarzenegger."

[SOUNDBITE: Webb on the weight room, 2026 podcast; exact words checked against the clip]

Number 4. His body. The Achilles surgery. Coach after coach, chasing that 2007 magic again.

## 9. So what y'all think?
Now I'ma keep it real with y'all.

Alan Webb ain't a failure. American record. US champion. Olympian.

But he was supposed to be the guy. The one who finally beat the world.

And he never won 1 medal.

Today he's coaching college runners at Ave Maria University in Florida.

And that high school record? This summer, a kid from Utah went after it at the Pre Classic. Came up short.

25 years later… it's still his.

So what y'all think? Was it the hype? The training? Or did he just peak in high school?
