Title idea: "Is This The End Of Mondo Duplantis?" Facts + sources in research.md.
Structure (new, not the usual format): cold open on the Stockholm loss → the streak nobody could stop → the summer it stopped →
the wedding question → the injury → the man from Greece (inconsistent, then building) → why the gap is closing → the question.
About 1,150 words. Read each part in one take; if you mess up, pause and say the line again.

## 1. Stockholm
June 7, 2026. Stockholm, Sweden. His home meet. His family in the stands.

For almost 3 years, Mondo Duplantis did not lose. 40 meets in a row. Nobody touched him.

And tonight… he can't get over 6 meters.

[SOUNDBITE: the miss / crowd groan, Stockholm 2026]

Kurtis Marschall from Australia clears 5.90. Mondo is stuck at 5.80.

He lost. At home.

And that wasn't the last time this year.

So is this the beginning of the end for the greatest pole vaulter who ever lived? 'Cause there's a man from Greece who's been waitin' on this moment his whole life.

## 2. The streak
To understand why this is a big deal, you gotta understand what Mondo been doing.

He broke the world record for the first time in 2020. He was 20 years old.

And then he just… kept going. 1 centimeter at a time.

Every time he breaks it, he only goes up 1 centimeter. People say that's on purpose. So he can break it again.

July 24, 2022. World Championships, Eugene. 6.21. World record.

2023. The Prefontaine Classic. 6.23. World record.

2024. Xiamen. 6.24. Then the Olympic final in Paris. 6.25. Then Poland. 6.26.

2025. Stockholm, 6.28. Budapest, 6.29. And in Tokyo at the World Championships… 6.30.

[SOUNDBITE: crowd at the 6.30, Tokyo 2025]

4 summers in a row. Outdoors. A world record every single summer.

And in 2025 he didn't lose one time. 16 meets. 16 wins.

## 3. The summer it stopped
Now look at 2026.

He did set a record in March. 6.31. But that was indoors. And in track and field, the outdoor season is what everybody really cares about.

And outdoors this year?

He tried 6.32 in Lausanne. Missed. Zurich. Missed. Budapest. Missed. And again at the Ultimate Championship. Missed.

His best jump all summer was 6.25.

For the first time since 2021… a whole outdoor season went by and Mondo Duplantis did not break the world record.

## 4. The wedding
And here's the part people been talkin' about.

5 days after that loss in Stockholm… Mondo got married.

June 12. A big wedding in the south of France. His longtime girlfriend, Desiré Inglander.

Right after the loss he even joked about it.

[SOUNDBITE: Mondo, Stockholm, "unlucky in sports – lucky in love"]

Now y'all know what they say. When a man gets married… the hunger changes. Life changes.

Is that what happened to Mondo? Or is it just a coincidence?

He said himself he felt "unfocused" that night in Stockholm. And he skipped the very next Diamond League meet in Oslo.

I'll let y'all decide.

## 5. The leg
July. London Diamond League.

Mondo clears 5.95. Then something don't feel right. His left thigh.

He stops. He just… stops jumping.

[SOUNDBITE: Mondo, London, "It felt really tight… It got a lot worse on the next jump."]

Sam Kendricks wins. Mondo's second again.

Then September. The Diamond League Final in Brussels. The trophy he had won 5 years in a row.

He's warming up… and the same leg flares up.

He pulls out.

[SOUNDBITE: Mondo, Brussels, "It's the same sensation that I had in London… I had to make a very difficult decision to pull out."]

So who wins the trophy that night?

The man from Greece.

## 6. The man from Greece
His name is Emmanouil Karalis. Everybody calls him Manolo.

He's from Athens. Born October 20, 1999. That's 3 weeks before Mondo. Same age. They been jumping against each other since they were teenagers.

His dad was a decathlete. His dad is still his coach.

And here go the crazy part. Karalis has beat Mondo at a world championship before. 2018. World Indoors. They were both 18.

But after that, Karalis could not get it together.

2022 World Championships. He don't even make the final. 22nd.

2022 Europeans. Out in qualifying again.

2023 World Championships. He don't clear a single bar. No height.

He's got all the talent in the world. But when it counts… he disappears.

## 7. The build
Then something changes.

2024. Paris Olympics. Bronze.

2025. World Championships in Tokyo. Silver. Right behind Mondo.

And this year? February, he clears 6.17 indoors.

Then August. Lausanne. 6.16. That same night Mondo goes 6.21. It's the first time in history 2 men cleared 6.16 in the same competition.

[SOUNDBITE: Karalis clears 6.16, Lausanne]

1 week later. Zurich. Karalis clears 6.20.

[SOUNDBITE: Karalis 6.20, Zurich, crowd]

6.20. That makes him the second-best pole vaulter in history. Higher than Renaud Lavillenie. Higher than Sergey Bubka ever went.

Then he even took 3 tries at the world record. 6.32.

And then Brussels. Mondo's hurt. Karalis wins his first Diamond League trophy. 6.12. A meet record.

[SOUNDBITE: Karalis, Brussels, "I am living my dreams"]

## 8. The gap
Now let's keep it real.

Mondo still won every championship this year. World Indoors. Europeans. The Ultimate Championship.

And Karalis has not beaten him since 2018.

But look at who came second in all 3 of those. Karalis. Every time.

And here's the thing. Karalis still ain't consistent when it matters.

At the Europeans this year, the same month he jumped 6.16 and 6.20… he could only clear 6.00 for the title.

Early this season, he was 7th in Shanghai. 4th in Oslo. He said himself he wasn't believing in himself.

So imagine when he figures it out. When he shows up at a championship and jumps like he did in Zurich.

In Lausanne, the gap was 5 centimeters. In Zurich? 5 centimeters.

## 9. The question
And there's one more thing.

In October, Mondo announced he's skipping the whole 2027 indoor season. He said he had too much going on and not enough time to prepare. His body needs to recover.

Now don't get it twisted. Mondo is still the best in the world. He's 27. He still holds the world record. And he might come back in 2027 and break it 3 more times.

But for the first time in his career… he lost more than we've seen in years. He got hurt. The world record didn't fall. And there's a man from Greece standing right there.

So y'all tell me. Is this the end of Mondo Duplantis? Or is Karalis about to find out what happens when you wake up a champion?
