## 01 · Cold open
August eighth, twenty twenty-four. Paris. Olympic two hundred final.

On the line, a twenty-one-year-old from a small village in Botswana.

On his spikes? A date. His momma's birthday.

Nineteen forty-six. African record. Olympic gold.

The first Olympic gold medal his whole country ever won.

And the woman whose birthday was on them shoes? She ain't live to see it. She passed away less than three months before.

This the rise of Letsile Tebogo.

## 02 · Kanye, Botswana
Letsile Tebogo born June seventh, two thousand three, in Kanye, Botswana. A village about an hour outside the capital.

His momma, Seratiwa, raised him. Grandma, uncles, aunties helped too. Folks back home call him "School-boy."

And here's the thing about Tebogo. He never left.

Most African sprinters that get good, they leave. They go to American colleges, Europe, wherever the money at.

Not him. He stayed home. Same coach, Kebonyemodisa Mosimanyane, on Botswana tracks. Still to this day.

## 03 · The kid gets fast
Twenty twenty-one. He seventeen. First time ever runnin' for Botswana overseas.

That same summer, World Under-Twenty Championships in Nairobi. He win the hundred.

Then twenty twenty-two. Back home in Gaborone, he run nine ninety-six.

First man from Botswana ever to break ten seconds. And he still a teenager.

That summer in Cali, Colombia, World Under-Twenties again. Nine ninety-one. World under-twenty record.

And watch what he do. He ain't even at the line yet, and he already celebratin'. Lookin' back, arms out, smilin'.

The whole internet said the same thing: that's young Bolt.

Some folks called it showboatin'. He ain't care. He was havin' fun.

## 04 · Budapest: the grown men
Twenty twenty-three. Now he racin' grown men. World Championships, Budapest.

Hundred meter final. Nine eighty-eight. Silver. Only man in front of him? Noah Lyles.

Five days later, two hundred. Bronze.

Twenty years old, two world medals. He ain't the kid no more.

And early twenty twenty-four, he show y'all the range. Three hundred meters in thirty sixty-nine. A world best.

Four hundred in forty-four twenty-nine.

Paris comin'. And he lookin' like the guy.

## 05 · May 2024
Then May nineteenth, twenty twenty-four.

Tebogo in the U S for a race. And back home, his momma pass away.

And the way he found out was cold. The news hit social media before the family could even tell him.

He said it plain. "When my mother passed away, I thought it was the end of my career."

He thought about walkin' away. From all of it.

But he ain't. He came back to the track. Got a tattoo for her. And he went to Paris carryin' her with him.

Her birthday on his spikes. Her initials painted on his nails.

## 06 · Paris
Paris, the hundred first. Nine eighty-six. National record. But sixth.

Then the two hundred. August eighth.

Kenny Bednarek in there. Noah Lyles in there.

Tebogo come off that curve and it's over. Nineteen forty-six.

African record. Gold.

Botswana ain't never won an Olympic gold medal. Ever. Till that night.

Back home they lost it. The government gave the whole country the afternoon off.

Over thirty thousand people packed the national stadium to welcome him home. Cash. Cattle. Reports say even houses.

He wasn't done neither. Four-by-four silver with Botswana. Another African record.

And at the end of the year? World Athletics Male Athlete of the Year.

From Kanye to the top of the whole sport.

## 07 · Being the guy
Now here's the part nobody tells you. Bein' the guy is heavy.

February twenty twenty-five, floods hit Gaborone. And Tebogo out there in the water, pullin' stranded cars out the flood. An Olympic champ.

Then Tokyo. World Championships. Hundred meter final. False start. Disqualified.

Two hundred? Fourth. Nineteen sixty-five. Off the podium.

But then the four-by-four. Tebogo run the second leg, and Botswana win gold. World champs.

He ain't win it by himself. But he ain't got to no more.

## 08 · 2026
Twenty twenty-six start rough. He go down in warm-ups, get helped off the track. Miss the race.

Then he come back. Win in Oslo. Win in Lausanne.

And in May, the World Relays come to Gaborone. His home. His people in the stands.

Four-by-four. Tebogo on the second leg. Gold. African record. Third fastest ever run.

Then September, Budapest. The new Ultimate Championship. The same city where he won his first world medals.

Hundred? Seventh. Two hundred? Second, nineteen seventy-six.

And after, he said somethin' real. "I carry so many hopes and lives behind my back, so it is just too much. But we will go through it."

## 09 · Close
So that's Letsile Tebogo. Twenty-three years old.

From a village in Botswana, to the fastest teenager on the planet, to Olympic champion, just months after losin' his momma.

He never left home. He carried his momma to Paris. And now he carryin' a whole country.

The question ain't if he fast. We know he fast.

The question is how long one man can carry all that.
