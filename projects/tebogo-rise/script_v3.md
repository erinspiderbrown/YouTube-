## 01 · Cold open
Look. For like a hundred years, the sprints been the same thing.

America. Jamaica. Maybe Canada, maybe Great Britain.

Africa? Nah. Africa was distance. Africa was the marathon.

Last time a African man won a Olympic sprint? Nineteen-oh-eight.

Then August eighth, twenty twenty-four. Paris. Olympic two hundred final.

Twenty-one-year-old from a lil village in Botswana. Nineteen forty-six.

Gold. First African man ever to win the Olympic two hundred.

And in three years, Let-see-lay Tuhbowho went from a kid at the World Under-Twenties to the dude that changed what a whole continent think is possible.

## 02 · Who he is
Let-see-lay Tuhbowho. Born June seventh, two thousand three. Kanye, Botswana.

Lil village, 'bout a hour outside Gaborone.

He was playin' soccer first. Track wasn't even the plan.

His momma, Seratiwa, raised him. She seen how fast he was and told him, go all in.

He picked track in twenty nineteen. Same coach then, same coach now.

And he ain't never leave. No college in America. No Jamaica. He stayed home.

## 02b · Botswana before him
Now peep where Botswana was before him.

Country under three million people. Most of it desert.

Before Paris, Botswana had two Olympic medals. Two. Ever.

Nijel Amos, eight hundred silver, London twenty twelve. And a four-by-four bronze in Tokyo.

One world champ. Amantle Montsho, women's four hundred, twenty eleven.

But a real sprinter? A hundred, two hundred dude? They ain't never had one.

That's where he came from.

## 03 · The World Under-Twenties
Twenty twenty-one. Nairobi, Kinya. World Under-Twenties.

Eighteen years old. He win the hundred.

Folks in track start sayin' his name. But twenty twenty-two, that's when everybody found out.

April, back home in Gaborone. Nine ninety-six.

First dude from Botswana ever under ten. And that's a world under-twenty record.

July, his first grown-man Worlds, in Eugene. In the heats? Nine ninety-four. Against grown men.

Then August. Cali, Colombia. World Under-Twenties again.

Hundred final. He cookin' everybody. And before he even get to the line, he turn around. Arms out. Smilin'.

Nine ninety-one. World under-twenty record.

Some folks called it showboatin'. The internet called him somethin' else. Young Bolt.

But here go what people missed. He wasn't just a fast kid. He was a fast kid from a country that ain't never had one.

## 04 · Budapest 2023
So now the question. Can the junior king hang with grown men?

Twenty twenty-three. World Championships. Budapest.

Hundred final. Noah Lyles in there. Best in the world.

Tuhbowho run nine eighty-eight. National record. Silver.

Just like that, first African man ever with a world medal in the hundred.

Five days later, the two hundred. And he hidin' somethin'.

In the semi, he tore somethin'. Grade-one tear.

Ran the final anyway. Bronze.

Twenty years old. Hurt. Two world medals.

One year after the junior meet, he on the big podium twice. That ain't supposed to happen that fast.

## 05 · Road to Paris
Early twenty twenty-four, he show y'all he ain't just a hundred, two hundred dude.

Three hundred meters. Thirty sixty-nine. World best.

Four hundred. Forty-four twenty-nine.

Paris comin'. And for the first time, Africa got a real sprint favorite.

[quiet, respectful]
Then May nineteenth, twenty twenty-four.

He in the U S for a race. Back home, his momma passed.

And he found out the worst way. It was on social media 'fore the family could even call him.

He said it himself. "When my mother passed away, I thought it was the end of my career."

He thought about walkin' away.

But he came back. Got a tattoo for her. And he took her to Paris with him.

Her birthday on his spikes. Her initials on his nails.

## 06 · Paris 2024
Paris. Hundred first. Nine eighty-six, national record. Sixth.

Then August eighth. Two hundred final.

Kenny Bednarek. Noah Lyles. The Americans that run this.

Africa been close before. Frankie Fredericks, Namibia? Four Olympic silvers in the nineties. Never gold.

Tuhbowho come off the curve. And it's over.

Nineteen forty-six. African record. Fifth fastest ever.

He beat his chest crossin' the line.

First African man ever to win the Olympic two hundred. And Botswana first Olympic gold. Ever.

He said, "It means a lot to the African continent, 'cause now they see Africa as a sprinting home."

## 07 · Africa
Back home they shut it down. Government gave the whole country the afternoon off.

Over thirty thousand people packed the national stadium just to welcome him home.

And it ain't just Botswana. All over Africa, folks felt that.

'Cause for years it was, you African and you fast? Go run the marathon. Four hundred at most.

He said he wanted to change that. His words: "Africans can be sprinters too."

And he did it his way. Ain't move to America. Ain't move to Jamaica. Did it from home.

End of that year: World Athletics Male Athlete of the Year.

## 08 · The generation
Now watch what happen next.

Twenty twenty-five. Worlds in Tokyo. He false-start in the hundred. Fourth in the two hundred.

But his teammate, Collen Kebinatshipi, twenty-one years old? He win the four hundred. Forty-three fifty-three. Botswana's first men's world champ.

Bayapo Ndori, another one from Botswana? Bronze.

Then the four-by-four. Tuhbowho on the second leg. Botswana. World champs.

Twenty twenty-six. World Relays come to Africa for the first time. Gaborone, Botswana.

He out there before the meet doin' kids' track with the lil ones.

Then the four-by-four, in front of his own people. Gold. African record.

And all over the continent, the young sprinters keep comin'. Nigeria's Kanyinsola Ajayi this year, beatin' the pros in the Diamond League.

Africa a sprinting home now. Just like he said.

## 09 · The weight
But bein' the face of a whole continent? That's heavy.

This September, after a loss in Budapest, he said, "I carry so many hopes and lives behind my back, so it is just too much. But we will go through it."

## 10 · Close
So that's Let-see-lay Tuhbowho.

World Under-Twenty champ at eighteen.

Two world medals at twenty.

Olympic champ at twenty-one.

First African man to win the Olympic two hundred.

He ran for his momma. He ran for Botswana. And he ended up runnin' for all of Africa.

Now it's kids from Kanye to Lagos that don't think they gotta run the marathon. They think they can be him.
