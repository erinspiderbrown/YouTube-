## 01 · Cold open
For over a hundred years, the sprints been the same story.

America. Jamaica. Maybe Canada, maybe Great Britain.

Africa? Africa was distance. Africa was marathons.

The last time an African man won an Olympic sprint title, it was nineteen-oh-eight.

Then August eighth, twenty twenty-four. Paris. Olympic two hundred final.

A twenty-one-year-old from a village in Botswana. Nineteen forty-six.

Gold. The first African man to ever win the Olympic two hundred.

And in three years, Letsile Tebogo went from a kid at the World Under-Twenties to the man who changed what a whole continent believe is possible.

## 02 · Who he is
Letsile Tebogo born June seventh, two thousand three, in Kanye, Botswana. A village about an hour outside the capital.

He played soccer first. Track wasn't even the plan.

His momma, Seratiwa, raised him. And when she saw how fast he was, she told him go all in.

He picked athletics in twenty nineteen. Same coach then, same coach now: Kebonyemodisa Mosimanyane.

And he never left home. No American college. No Jamaica. He stayed in Botswana.

## 03 · The World Under-Twenties: he arrives
Twenty twenty-one. Nairobi, Kenya. World Under-Twenty Championships.

Seventeen years old. He win the hundred.

People in track start sayin' his name. But twenty twenty-two is when the world found out.

April, back home in Gaborone. Nine ninety-six.

First man from Botswana ever under ten seconds. And it's a world under-twenty record.

July, his first senior Worlds in Eugene. In the heats? Nine ninety-four. Faster than his own junior record. Against grown men.

Then August, Cali, Colombia. World Under-Twenties again.

Hundred meter final. He blow the field away. And before he even hit the line, he turn around. Arms out. Smilin'. Celebratin'.

Nine ninety-one. World under-twenty record. Still the record today.

Folks called it showboatin'. The whole internet called it somethin' else. Young Bolt.

But here's what people missed. He wasn't just a fast kid. He was a fast kid from a country that never had one.

## 04 · Budapest 2023: one year later, two world medals
So now the question: can the junior king run with grown men?

Twenty twenty-three. World Championships. Budapest.

Hundred meter final. Noah Lyles, the best in the world, in the lane.

Tebogo run nine eighty-eight. National record. Silver.

And just like that, he the first African man ever to win a world medal in the hundred.

Five days later, the two hundred. And he got a secret.

In the semifinal, he tore somethin'. A grade-one tear.

He ran the final anyway. Bronze.

Twenty years old. Hurt. Two world medals.

A year after the junior championships, he on the senior podium twice. That ain't supposed to happen that fast.

## 05 · The road to Paris
Early twenty twenty-four, he show y'all he ain't just a hundred and two hundred guy.

Three hundred meters, thirty sixty-nine. A world best.

Four hundred, forty-four twenty-nine.

Paris comin'. And for the first time ever, Africa got a real sprint favorite.

[quiet, respectful]
Then May nineteenth, twenty twenty-four.

Tebogo in the U S for a race. Back home, his momma pass away.

And he found out the worst way. It was on social media before the family could even reach him.

He said it himself. "When my mother passed away, I thought it was the end of my career."

He thought about walkin' away.

But he came back. Got a tattoo for her. And he took her to Paris with him.

Her birthday on his spikes. Her initials painted on his nails.

## 06 · Paris 2024
Paris. The hundred first. Nine eighty-six, national record. Sixth.

Then August eighth. The two hundred final.

Kenny Bednarek. Noah Lyles. The Americans that run this event.

Africa had come close before. Frankie Fredericks from Namibia? Four Olympic silvers in the nineties. Never gold.

Tebogo come off the curve. And it's over.

Nineteen forty-six. African record. Fifth fastest man in history.

He beat his chest crossin' the line.

The first African man ever to win the Olympic two hundred. And Botswana's first Olympic gold medal. Ever.

He said, "It means a lot to the African continent, 'cause now they see Africa as a sprinting home."

## 07 · Africa
Back home they shut it down. The government gave the whole country the afternoon off.

Over thirty thousand people packed the national stadium to welcome him home.

But it wasn't just Botswana. All over Africa, folks felt that one.

'Cause for years the message was, if you African and you fast, you run the marathon. Or the four hundred at most.

He said he wanted to change that. In his words: "Africans can be sprinters too."

And he did it his way. He ain't move to America. He ain't move to Jamaica. He did it from home.

End of that year: World Athletics Male Athlete of the Year.

## 08 · The generation
And watch what happen next.

Twenty twenty-five. World Championships, Tokyo. Tebogo false-start in the hundred. Fourth in the two hundred.

But his teammate, Collen Kebinatshipi, twenty-one years old. He win the four hundred. Forty-three fifty-three. Botswana's first men's world champion.

Bayapo Ndori, another one from Botswana? Bronze.

Then the four-by-four. Tebogo on the second leg. Botswana. World champions.

Twenty twenty-six. The World Relays come to Africa for the first time ever. Gaborone, Botswana.

Tebogo out there before the meet doin' kids' athletics with the little ones.

Then the four-by-four, in front of his own people. Gold. African record.

And around the continent, the young sprinters keep comin'. Nigeria's Kanyinsola Ajayi this year, beatin' the pros in the Diamond League.

Africa a sprinting home now. Just like he said.

## 09 · The weight
But bein' the face of a continent is heavy.

This September, after a loss in Budapest, he said, "I carry so many hopes and lives behind my back, so it is just too much. But we will go through it."

## 10 · Close
So that's Letsile Tebogo.

World Under-Twenty champ at seventeen.

Two world medals at twenty.

Olympic champion at twenty-one.

The first African man to win the Olympic two hundred.

He ran for his momma. He ran for Botswana. And he ended up runnin' for all of Africa.

Now there's kids from Kanye to Lagos who don't think they gotta run the marathon. They think they can be him.
