## 1. Erriyon Knighton (sprints, Tampa FL): score 10/10

**Hook:** He turned pro at 16, broke two of Usain Bolt's records as a teenager, and won world medals. Then a positive test for a steroid used in cattle got him banned until 2029.
**Title:** "The Teen Who Broke Usain Bolt's Record... What Went Wrong?" (alt: "America's Fastest Teenager Ever... What Went Wrong?")

**Peak (verified)**
- Born January 29, 2004. Turned pro with Adidas in January 2021 at age 16, giving up his last 2 years at Hillsborough High. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erriyon_Knighton
- At the 2021 U.S. Olympic Trials he ran 19.88 in the semi (June 26) and 19.84 in the final (June 27). Both broke Bolt's world U20 record. Source: Wikipedia above, and NBC Sports video below.
- Tokyo 2021 Olympics, 200: 4th in 19.93, at age 17.
- Ran 19.49 in Baton Rouge on April 30, 2022. It was 4th-fastest ever when he ran it; Wikipedia now lists him **6th** all-time. The mark was **not ratified** as a U20 record because the anti-doping testing requirements weren't met. Do not call it "the world U20 record" without that caveat.
- World Championships 200: bronze in 2022 (19.80), silver in 2023 (19.75). National 200 title in 2023, beating Fred Kerley.

**What went wrong**
- **Positive test.** On March 26, 2024 USADA provisionally suspended him for a metabolite of trenbolone (epitrenbolone). In June 2024 a USADA arbitrator accepted his contaminated-meat (oxtail) explanation and found "no fault or negligence", with no ban. He ran the Paris 2024 Olympics and finished 4th in 19.99.
- **Appeal.** The AIU/World Athletics and WADA appealed to CAS.
- **CAS ruling, September 12, 2025.** CAS partly upheld the appeals and imposed a **4-year ban**. Credit was given for the provisional suspension (April 12 to June 19, 2024). Results from March 26 to April 12, 2024 were disqualified. CAS found the oxtail explanation not proven. On October 21, 2025 the AIU said the violation was ruled **intentional**. Sources:
  - https://citiusmag.com/articles/erriyon-knighton-banned-cas-doping-violation-2025
  - https://africa.espn.com/olympics/trackandfield/story/_/id/46232358/sprinter-erriyon-knighton-gets-4-year-ban-positive-doping-test
  - CAS award PDF: https://www.tas-cas.org/fileadmin/user_upload/10800_10802_Award_FINAL__for_publ._.pdf
- **When the ban ends.** Wikipedia and most reports say July 2029. One outlet says October 2029 (**the sources conflict**; say "until 2029"). He misses the 2028 LA Olympics.
- On April 12, 2026 Gout Gout (Australia) took over the U20 200 record (per Wikipedia).

**Status, late 2026:** serving the ban. I found no report of an appeal to the Swiss Federal Tribunal (**UNCONFIRMED** either way).

## 2. Mary Cain (middle distance, Bronxville NY): score 9/10

**Hook:** At 17 she was the youngest American ever on a World Championships team. Then she joined Nike's Oregon Project under Alberto Salazar, and her body broke down.
**Title:** "The Fastest Girl in America... What Happened to Mary Cain?" (her own NYT headline was "I Was the Fastest Girl in America, Until I Joined Nike")

**Peak (verified):** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Cain_(athlete)
- Born May 3, 1996.
- May 17, 2013: ran 1500 m in 4:04.62, a U.S. high school and junior record.
- June 2013: ran 800 m in 1:59.51, the first American high school, junior or youth girl under 2:00.
- August 2013: at 17, the youngest American ever to make a World Championships team. She was also the youngest athlete to reach a World Championships 1500 m final.
- November 20, 2013: joined the Nike Oregon Project and skipped college.
- January to February 2014: world junior indoor 1000 m record (2:35.80).
- July 2014: won World Junior 3000 m gold in Eugene.

**What went wrong**
- From 2015 her results declined: 8th at the 2015 USAs and 11th at the 2016 Trials.
- In her own account she had RED-S, lost her period for about 3 years, and had 5 bone fractures.
- **November 7, 2019:** her NYT video op-ed (20M+ views) said she was "emotionally and physically abused by a system designed by Alberto and endorsed by Nike."
- **Salazar:** He got a 4-year USADA anti-doping ban (September 30, 2019). These were not Cain's violations, and the ban concerned other athletes. In 2021 the U.S. Center for SafeSport banned him for life.
- **Lawsuit:** On October 11, 2021 she sued Nike and Salazar for $20M. They settled on November 27, 2023; the terms are confidential. Sources: Wikipedia above, and https://www.axios.com/runner-mary-cain-sues-nike-former-coach-alleged-abuse-cf2a892e-657e-4ec8-ad5f-213fdd192cce.html

**Status, late 2026**
- Her memoir *This Is Not About Running* came out in 2026. It was excerpted by NYRR in June 2026 and reviewed by Yahoo Sports: https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/runner-mary-cain-memoir-delves-150000433.html
- She founded Atalanta NYC, which mentors Bronx girls through running.
- She is described as a Stanford medical student who runs for fun, per a May 2026 podcast and an NYRR June 2026 profile. **Med school is from 2 sources but not first-party**; confirm before stating.
- Wikipedia says she became eligible to represent **Ireland** from February 16, 2024 (**UNCONFIRMED** whether she has raced for Ireland).

## 4. Alan Webb (mile, Reston VA): score 8/10

**Hook:** His high school mile record (3:53.43) has stood for 25 years. He broke the American record too, but never won a global medal, and his triathlon pivot flopped.
**Title:** "America's Greatest High School Miler Ever... What Went Wrong?"

**Peak (verified):** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Webb_(runner)
- January 2001: ran 3:59.86 indoors, the first U.S. high schooler under 4:00 indoors.
- **May 27, 2001:** ran **3:53.43** at the Prefontaine Classic, breaking Jim Ryun's 36-year-old high school record. It still stands.
- He went to Michigan for 1 year, then turned pro with Nike in 2002.
- He won the 2004 Olympic Trials 1500 but went out in the first round in Athens.
- **July 21, 2007:** ran 3:46.91 in Brasschaat, an American record that lasted until Yared Nuguse in September 2023.

**What went wrong**
- Webb was 8th in the 2007 Worlds final. He was 5th at the 2008 Trials and missed Beijing.
- He moved to Salazar in 2009, had Achilles surgery in 2010, and left Salazar in 2011.
- He retired from track on February 15, 2014 after Millrose.
- His triathlon career went nowhere: he crashed on the bike in his debut, and World Triathlon's last listed result is a DNF in 2015. Source: https://www.220triathlon.com/news/alan-webb-crashes-in-triathlon-debut-at-pan-american-champs
- He says himself that he got "weight room" training wrong ("went full Arnold Schwarzenegger"). Source: May 2026 Running Effect podcast below.

**Status, late 2026:** head cross country and track coach at Ave Maria University in Florida (Wikipedia, and he says so in the May 2026 podcast). Wikipedia flags the article as needing citations, so **confirm on the Ave Maria staff page**.

## 6. Marion Jones (sprints, Thousand Oaks CA): score 8/10

**Hook:** She was a 4-time state 100 m champion in high school and won 5 medals in Sydney. Then she lied about steroids and went to federal prison.

- January 11, 2008: sentenced to **6 months**. She served it at FMC Carswell (Fort Worth) from March to September 5, 2008.
- She later played in the WNBA for the Tulsa Shock (2010–11, 47 games).

**Status, late 2026:** lives in Austin, Texas. Speaker, coach, and runs "Driven Performance" (2024), per Wikipedia. No 2025–26 news was checked in depth (**re-verify**).

## 7. Brianna Rollins-McNeal (100 m hurdles, Miami FL / Clemson): score 7/10

**Hook:** As a Clemson junior she ran the fastest collegiate hurdles time ever, then won world and Olympic gold. Two bans later, she missed 2 Olympics and came back in 2026 with no sponsor.
**Title:** "Fastest College Hurdler Ever... Then Banned 5 Years" (alt: "Olympic Champion... What Went Wrong?")

**Peak (verified):** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brianna_Rollins-McNeal
- June 2013: NCAA record of 12.39 for Clemson. She turned pro afterwards.
- 2013 world champion (12.44).
- 2016 Rio Olympic champion (12.48), leading a U.S. 1-2-3 sweep.

**What went wrong (exact rulings)**
- **2017:** a 1-year ban for 3 whereabouts failures in 12 months (the third was September 27, 2016). It was backdated to December 19, 2016, and she missed the 2017 Worlds. There was **no positive test**.
- **June 2021:** a **5-year ban for tampering** with the results-management process. She had submitted altered dates on medical documents explaining a January 2020 missed test, which happened while she was recovering from a pregnancy termination procedure. It was backdated to August 15, 2020. CAS upheld it on July 2, 2021, and she missed Tokyo and Paris. She was **never accused of using a banned substance**. Sources:
  - https://www.espn.com/olympics/trackandfield/story/_/id/31750141/olympic-champion-brianna-mcneal-loses-appeal-5-year-ban
  - https://www.olympics.com/en/news/olympic-champ-brianna-mcneal-appeals-five-year-anti-doping-suspension

**Status, late 2026:** The ban ended in August 2025. She returned in 2026: two 12.9s in Austin and a windy 12.88 at the Ed Murphey Classic. She joined Bandit's "Unsponsored Project" before the 2026 USAs. She had 2 children during the ban. Source: https://citiusmag.com/articles/brianna-rollins-mcneal-bandit-unsponsored-project-2026 (July 20, 2026)

**Footage (not FloSports)**

**Commons photos:** yes, 8 files. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Brianna_Rollins

## 9. Mary Decker (middle distance, CA/OR): score 7/10

**Hook:** She set a world record at 14 and was America's sweetheart. Then came the 1984 Olympic fall and, 12 years later, a testosterone ban.
**Title:** "America's Teenage World-Record Holder... What Went Wrong?"

**Peak (verified):** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Decker
- 1973: first world record, an indoor mile in 4:40.1, while she was a young teenager. Wikipedia doesn't state her age; she was born August 4, 1958, which makes her 14 or 15. **Check the exact age before saying "at 14".**
- 1974: indoor world records at 800 m and 880 y.
- 1982: 6 world records.
- 1983: won both the 1500 and 3000 at the Helsinki Worlds.
- 1985: mile world record of 4:16.71.

**What went wrong**
- Missed 1976 (stress fractures) and 1980 (boycott).
- **1984 LA 3000 final:** collided with Zola Budd and fell. Budd was DQ'd, then reinstated.
- 1988: no medal.
- **1996:** her June Trials sample showed a testosterone/epitestosterone ratio above 6:1. She was banned by the IAAF in June 1997. In April 1999 an IAAF arbitration panel upheld a **retroactive 2-year ban** from June 17, 1996, which cost her the 1997 World Indoor silver. Her U.S. lawsuit was dismissed for lack of jurisdiction (7th Circuit, 2001).
- She retired in 1999 after more than 30 orthopedic procedures.
- She has always denied doping. **Wikipedia's timeline mixes up the order** of the 1999 USATF and IAAF rulings, so check against a primary report.

**Status, late 2026:** retired since 1999 and lives in Eugene, Oregon. She appeared in the 2016 documentary *The Fall*. No recent news was checked.

## 10. Jordan Hasay (distance, San Luis Obispo CA): score 6/10

**Hook:** She ran the Olympic Trials at 16 and broke the high school record, then ran the second-fastest marathon ever by an American woman. Since then she has been slowed by injuries and the Salazar fallout.
**Title:** "America's Next Great Distance Runner... What Happened to Jordan Hasay?"

**Peak (verified):** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Hasay
- Won Foot Locker cross country as a freshman.
- July 4, 2008: high school 1500 record of 4:14.50 at the Olympic Trials, at 16.
- At Oregon she was an 18-time All-American and won NCAA indoor titles in the mile and 3000.
- 2017: marathon debut 3rd at Boston (2:23:00), then 3rd at Chicago in **2:20:57** (2nd-fastest American woman at the time).

**What went wrong**
- She joined the Nike Oregon Project in 2013. Salazar's ban in 2019 shut the NOP down. She has not been accused of any violation; do not imply otherwise.
- Stress fractures kept her out of most of 2018 (Runner's World 2019 interview below).
- Her results faded after 2019.

**Status, late 2026**
- She married and started a coaching business in Mechanicsburg, PA in 2023 (Wikipedia).
- She was due to return at the Boston 10K for Women on October 11, 2025 but was pulled from the field with an injury. **The source is a LetsRun forum post quoting the race release, so it's UNCONFIRMED**: https://www.letsrun.com/forum/flat_read.php?thread=13801358
- She has not retired officially. I found no 2026 news.
