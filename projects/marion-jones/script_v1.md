Style and settings, the same as the approved Pistorius and Drummond v4 reads:

- eleven_v4, stability 0.45, similarity 0.85.
- Target 165–170 wpm, about 8 minutes.

## 01 Cold open
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Aight, so check it. October 2007. White Plains, New York. A woman walk out a federal courthouse, step up to a wall of microphones… and she start cryin'.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Seven years before that, she was the fastest woman on the planet. Sydney Olympics. Five medals. Three of 'em gold. Her face was on magazine covers, commercials… everywhere.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And now she standin' there tellin' the whole world she lied.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Her name is Marion Jones. And three months after that press conference… a judge sent her to prison.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] So how do the biggest star in track go from five Olympic medals to a federal prison cell? Mane… lemme tell y'all.

## 02 The phenom
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Marion Jones was born in Los Angeles in 1975. Her mama was from Belize. And from jump, this girl was fast. Like, different fast.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] She won the California state title in the 100 four years straight. Four. Track and Field News named her the best high school athlete in the country… two years in a row.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] In 1992, at sixteen years old, she was at the Olympic Trials racin' grown women. She missed the 200 final by seven hundredths. They offered her a spot on the relay as a alternate… and she turned it down.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] She said when people come see her gold medals, she want to be able to say she ran for 'em. Sixteen years old. That's who she was.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] That same year, she missed a routine drug test. They hit her with a four-year ban. And her lawyer? Johnnie Cochran. Yeah… that Johnnie Cochran. He got the ban thrown out.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Then she went to North Carolina… on a basketball scholarship. And as a freshman, she won a national championship. Hoopin'. While bein' one of the fastest women in America.

## 03 The fastest woman alive
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] When she came back to track full time, it wasn't even close.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] 1997, world champion in the 100. 1999, world champion in the 100 again. She wasn't just winnin'… she was bullyin' the field.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And goin' into Sydney 2000, everybody knew what she was chasin'. Not one gold. Five medals.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Sydney. She win the 100 by damn near four tenths. Then she win the 200. Then she win gold in the 4 by 4. And she take bronze in the long jump and the 4 by 1.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Five medals at one Olympics. Ain't no woman ever done that in track and field before.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] But right in the middle of all that, somethin' happened that folks forgot about.

## 04 The warning signs
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Her husband at the time was C.J. Hunter. Shot putter. World champion. And durin' the Sydney Games, it come out he had failed four drug tests that summer. For a steroid.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] He was pulled from his role at the Games. Marion stood by him. And she said she had nothin' to do with none of it.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And folks believed her. Why wouldn't they? She was America's sweetheart.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Then 2003. Federal agents raid a lab in California called BALCO. And the whole thing started 'cause a coach sent a syringe to the anti-doping people… anonymously. A syringe full of a steroid that ain't show up on no test. They called it "the clear."

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] The coach who sent it? Trevor Graham. Marion Jones's old coach.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] In 2004, the BALCO founder, Victor Conte, went on national TV and said he gave Marion Jones banned drugs. She said he was lyin'. She sued him.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] By then she had a baby with Tim Montgomery… the 100 meter world record holder… who was caught up in BALCO too.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Every time somethin' came out, Marion had a answer. She never failed a test, she said. She was clean, she said. Over and over. For years.

## 05 The confession
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Until October 5th, 2007.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Marion Jones walked into federal court in White Plains and pleaded guilty. Two counts of lyin' to federal agents.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] One, about steroids. She admitted she took "the clear." She said her coach told her it was flaxseed oil. But she admitted that by November 2003 she knew what it really was… and she still looked federal agents in the face and said she ain't never used it.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] The second count was about a check fraud case tied to Tim Montgomery. She lied about that too.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Then she walked outside, stood in front of the cameras, and broke down. She said she was ashamed. She said she had betrayed everybody's trust. She said she let her country down.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] She retired from track that same day.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] She gave back all five Sydney medals. And in December, the IOC made it official. Every result from Sydney on… wiped. Like it never happened.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Even her relay teammates got stripped. They had to fight in court for years before they got theirs back.

## 06 Prison
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] January 2008. Sentencin'. Her lawyers asked for probation. Even the prosecutors said she came clean.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] The judge gave her six months. In federal prison.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] March 2008, the fastest woman in the world reported to a federal prison in Fort Worth, Texas. Carswell.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And it got worse in there. She say she got in a altercation with another inmate… and spent weeks in solitary confinement. Forty-nine days, by her count.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] September 2008, she walked out. Thirty-two years old. No medals. No records. No career.

## 07 After & ending
[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And here go the part people don't know. She ain't disappear.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] In 2010, at thirty-four years old, she made the WNBA. Tulsa Shock. Wasn't a star… but she made a pro roster, years after she last played basketball.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] She wrote a book. She started speakin' to young athletes… about choices. About what one lie can cost you.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] So where I'm at with it? I'ma keep it a buck. Marion Jones cheated. She admitted it. And she lied about it to everybody for years… to the fans, to her teammates, to federal agents.

[calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] But here's what get me. She ain't go to prison for the drugs. She went to prison for the lie.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] Sixteen years old, she turned down a Olympic relay spot 'cause she wanted every medal to be hers… earned.

[pause] [calm] [relaxed] [Texas accent] And in the end, not one of 'em was.
