## What his edit does
- **The footage IS the video.** Real broadcast footage, full screen, raw. No neon frames, no blurred backdrops, no HUD tags.
  Old 4:3 footage is just pillarboxed (black bars), not cropped or framed.
- **Real audio moments.** The narration stops and lets the moment play: crowd erupting, the commentator's call,
  the interview answer ("I think I just pushed it a little bit right"). Then he talks over the reaction.
- **Cold open = the moment itself.** "You see this guy right here?" over the footage, then the real audio of the
  peak, then the twist ("…except he wasn't"), then a hard cut to the story.
- **Cut pace:** a new shot every ~3–5 s (median 2.8 s). Hard cuts. No glitch/zoom transitions.
- **Graphics are few and clean:**
  - Big white number + name over darkened footage ("69 / MICHAEL JORDAN", "$1,000,000").
  - Clean white "document" cards: newspaper headlines, stat tables that slowly scroll/zoom, article screenshots.
  - Subject cut out on white with a one-line quote caption.
  - Small plain lower third for names ("Don Calhoun (Bulls fan)").
  - Occasional B&W + grain treatment on a scoreboard or face for a dramatic beat.
- **Motion:** slow push-ins, slow-motion and freeze on the key moment; punch-in on faces.
- **Sound:** music low and only between beats; risers and hits under reveals; silence before a punchline.
- **Narration:** conversational, punchy, rhetorical questions ("So what happens if…?"), a twist every minute.
