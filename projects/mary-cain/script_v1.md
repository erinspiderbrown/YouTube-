## 1. The video
November 7, 2019. A 23-year-old woman sits in front of a camera for 7 minutes.

No race. No medal. Just her talking.

That video has over 20 million views.

And it helped make sure the most powerful coach in running never coached again.

Her name is Mary Cain.

And 6 years before that video, she was the fastest girl America had ever seen.

## 2. Bronxville
Mary grew up in Bronxville, New York. Just outside the city.

As a freshman, she ran the 1500 in 4:17. A national record for a freshman.

By 16, she's running against grown women at the Olympic Trials.

And that's when a man named Alberto Salazar calls.

Salazar was the head coach of the Nike Oregon Project. Nike built that team to win Olympic medals, and he was the guy. Mo Farah. Galen Rupp. Gold medals.

He starts coaching Mary in October 2012. She's still in high school. Still living at home.

## 3. The rise
And at first? It works.

January 2013, she breaks the high school indoor mile record.

May 2013, she runs the 1500 in 4:04.62. American junior record.

June, at the Prefontaine Classic, the 800 in 1:59.51. Another record.

That summer she makes the World Championships in Moscow. She's 17. The youngest American ever to make a World Championships team.

And she makes the final.

Then in November, Mary makes the decision that changes everything. No college. She signs with Nike and goes pro. She's still a senior in high school.

## 4. The deal
In 2014 she's still winning. US indoor champion in the 1500. World Junior champion in the 3000.

On paper, she's the future of American running.

But that's not what was happening when the cameras were off.

In that video, Mary said the coaches wanted her thinner. And thinner. And thinner.

She said Salazar set a number for her. 114 pounds. And that she got weighed in front of her teammates.

She said there was talk of birth control pills and diuretics to drop the weight.

## 5. The body breaks
And her body started shutting down.

She said she lost her period for 3 years. She broke 5 bones.

Doctors have a name for it. RED-S. When you don't give your body enough fuel, your bones get weak and your hormones shut down.

The fastest girl in America couldn't stay healthy.

2015, she's 8th at USAs. 2016, the Olympic Trials, 11th.

And she said it got darker than that. She started cutting herself.

In 2016, she leaves Oregon and goes home to New York.

The girl who broke every record in the book just... disappeared.

## 6. The fall of Salazar
For 3 years, nobody really knew why.

Then September 30, 2019. USADA bans Alberto Salazar for 4 years for doping violations.

A couple weeks later, Nike shuts the Oregon Project down.

And 4 weeks after that, Mary posts the video.

Nike said these were "deeply troubling allegations" that she and her parents never raised before. They also said she was trying to get back on the team as recently as that April. Salazar denied it.

But Mary wasn't the only one. Sports Illustrated talked to 9 more former Oregon Project runners. And they backed her up.

## 7. The ban
2021, the US Center for SafeSport bans Salazar for life. For sexual and emotional misconduct.

Same year, Mary sues Salazar and Nike for $20 million.

November 2023, they settle. Nobody's saying for how much.

## 8. Where she at now
So where's Mary Cain now?

She started her own team in New York, Atalanta NYC. Women who run pro and mentor young girls in the city.

In 2024, she started running for Ireland.

April 2026, she drops her book. "This Is Not About Running."

And right now? She's in medical school. At Stanford.

## 9. The end
Mary Cain broke records at 16, ran a world final at 17, and turned pro before she finished high school.

She never won the Olympic medal everybody said she would.

But she might have done something bigger. The coach is banned for life. The team is gone. And everybody in this sport is watching how they treat the next 16-year-old girl.

Which one matters more: the medal she never won, or the video she did make?
