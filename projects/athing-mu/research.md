# Athing Mu (Athing Mu-Nikolayev): research file

Compiled 18 Nov 2026. Every fact has a source. Tags:
- **[CONFIRMED]**: two or more independent sources agree, or an official results page.
- **[ONE SOURCE]**: only one source found. Re-check before saying it on camera.
- **[CONFLICT]**: sources disagree. Both versions are given.
- **[UNCONFIRMED]**: could not verify. Do not use as fact.

LetsRun.com articles block automated fetching (HTTP 403). Facts credited to LetsRun come from search-engine summaries of those pages and from outlets that quote them (Pulse Sports, EssentiallySports, NBC, CITIUS MAG on X). Before the script is final, open the LetsRun links in a browser and check them.

**Corrections to the brief:**
- The youth coach was **Al Jennings**, not "Al Judkins".
- She was **16, not 17**, when she set the 600m record.
- **1:55.04** was her **2021** American record (Pre Classic, 21 Aug 2021). The 2023 record was **1:54.97**.
- The switch from Mallard to Kersee was in **Nov 2022**, not 2023.
- Budapest 2023 was a **bronze** (correct in the brief).

## 4. 2021 Olympic Trials and Tokyo

- Trials pro debut (rounds): FloTrack interview "Athing Mu Goes To Wrong Lane In Pro Debut" (25 Jun 2021). **FloTrack is banned, so use as a text source only.** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pnuwgh5rjAA

Silver Keely Hodgkinson (GBR) 1:55.88 (British record); bronze Raevyn Rogers (USA) 1:56.81. Youngest US woman to win an individual Olympic track title since **Wyomia Tyus (1964)**. CBS above; NBC Sports https://www.nbcsports.com/olympics/news/athing-mu-nikolayev-pregnant [CONFIRMED]
- **4x400 gold, Tokyo (final 7 Aug 2021): ran the 4th leg** (48.32 split per Wikipedia); team time 3:16.85. The team was Sydney McLaughlin, Allyson Felix, Dalilah Muhammad and Mu. Wikipedia [CONFIRMED for gold; split is ONE SOURCE]
- **21 Aug 2021, Prefontaine Classic, Eugene: 1:55.04, an American record** (and meet and U20 record), running alone over the last lap. https://www.letsrun.com/2021/08/athing-mu-caps-an-incredible-year-with-a-155-04-american-record/ ; NBC Sports YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2CTSlpKVHyg [CONFIRMED]
- 2021: won 35 of 36 races (including rounds). World Athletics Female Rising Star of the Year. Wikipedia (cites 12thman.com) [ONE SOURCE]

## 8. 2025: marriage and a comeback that fell short

## Story spine (turning points in order)

1. **Trenton, age 6**: a child of South Sudanese refugees follows her brothers to Trenton Track Club; never runs for her high school.
2. **24 Feb 2019, age 16**: 1:23.57 American indoor 600m record, beating an Olympian (Raevyn Rogers). "A star is born."
3. **2021, one season at Texas A&M**: collegiate records, NCAA 400 title (49.57). Turns pro (19 Jun) and signs with Nike.
4. **3 Aug 2021, Tokyo, age 19**: Olympic 800 gold in a US record 1:55.21, the first for a US woman since 1968. Plus 4x400 gold.
5. **24 Jul 2022, Eugene**: world champion by 0.08 s over Hodgkinson. The youngest ever to hold both titles. **Then (Nov 2022) she leaves her college coach for Bobby Kersee in LA.**
6. **2023, the doubt year**: admits thinking about quitting, nearly skips Worlds, loses for the first time (bronze, Budapest), then answers with a 1:54.97 American record three weeks later.
7. **24 Jun 2024, the fall**: torn hamstring weeks before, clips Rogers about 200m in, finishes last, appeal denied. Hodgkinson wins the gold Mu was defending (5 Aug).
8. **2025–26, the fade**: marries (Mar 2025), comeback stalls (last at Pre 2:03.44; out in the US semis 1 Aug 2025, her last race), quietly leaves Kersee for Dallas (Dec 2025), no races in 2026, **announces pregnancy 15 Oct 2026**. LA 2028 is an open question.

## Open questions (why she declined)

- **Physical or mental?** She points to the mind ("if you're not mentally in it, then your body won't be in it", 2025) and to "things lingering" from the fall. But she also had a hip problem (2022), a hamstring tear (2024), a second injury in Europe (2024) and "niggling injuries" (2025, ES). No full medical history is public.
- **Did the Kersee move hurt her?** Her best result under Kersee (1:54.97 AR, 2023) came *after* the switch, so the simple "the coach ruined her" story doesn't hold. Her decline came mainly 2024–25. It can't be proven either way.
- **Burnout / love of the sport:** she thought about quitting 3 times before 2023, has long wanted a modeling career, and joked about retiring early. How much she still wants elite racing is unclear. She says faith and marriage restored her love of track (2025), but results didn't follow.
- **Why exactly did she leave Kersee?** Only Kersee's account exists ("a life decision", Dallas). She hasn't spoken.
- **Who coaches her now?** LetsRun's anonymous source says there is a new coach; no name.
- **When is the baby due, and will she try for LA 2028?** Unknown. The Instagram "not done" comment is unverified wording.
- **Hamstring timing:** late April vs mid-May 2024. Her own accounts differ.
- **Engagement date:** July (Malibu) vs September 2024.
