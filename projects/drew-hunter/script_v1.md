# Drew Hunter: script v1 (user reads it)
Title: "America's Greatest High School Miler Ever... What Went Wrong?" (user pick, 24 Nov 2026)
Read it how you talk. Numbers are written the way you'd say them.

## Part 1
If you was watchin' high school track back in 2016, you already know the name Drew Hunter. This dude was not just good, he was the guy. He broke Alan Webb high school mile record, he was the first high schooler ever to break 8 minutes in the 3000, and everybody, I mean everybody, was sayin' he was gon' be the next great American miler. Every college wanted him, the shoe companies wanted him, and he had already committed to Oregon. And then he did somethin' nobody really did back then. He skipped college and went straight pro. That was 10 years ago. And here's the thing, in 10 years of bein' a pro, Drew Hunter has never made an Olympic team, and he never made a World Championship team on the track. So what happened? Let's get into it.

## Part 2
So first y'all gotta understand where he come from. Drew is from Purcellville, Virginia, he ran for Loudoun Valley High School, and running is literally the family business. His dad Marc ran 13:36 in the 5K back in the day, his mom Joan ran in college at West Virginia, and get this, his parents coached Alan Webb when Alan Webb was a freshman in high school. So Drew grew up around the same people that built the best high school miler America ever had. And honestly I feel like that's a lot of pressure for a kid, 'cause from day 1 people was already comparin' him to Webb.

## Part 3
And he lived up to it. January 2016 he runs 7:59 in the 3000 indoors, first high schooler ever under 8 minutes. A week later he at the Armory in New York and he runs 3:58 in the mile, and that broke Alan Webb indoor high school record, the guy his parents used to coach. Then he goes to the Penn Relays and does probably the craziest thing I ever seen a high schooler do. He get the baton on the anchor leg of the distance medley, like 50 meters behind, and he run them down. Ran about a 4 minute mile leg, won it for his school, and that clip got millions of views. He was Gatorade National Runner of the Year. On paper, this was the most complete high school distance runner we had seen since Webb.

## Part 4
So now the decision. Drew had already committed to Oregon. Oregon, the best running school in the country. And then in July 2016 he turned all that down and signed a 10 year deal with Adidas. 10 years. He was 18. And I'm not gon' lie, I understood it at the time. You a kid, somebody offer you a pro contract and a check, most people gon' take that. But a lot of people in the running world was nervous, 'cause they had already seen what happened to Alan Webb and Mary Cain, and they knew that turnin' pro at 18 don't always go how you think it's gon' go.

## Part 5
And for a while it actually looked like it was gon' work. He moved to Boulder, Colorado, and him and his boys started Tinman Elite, and they became like the cool new team in running, the merch, the vlogs, all of it. In February 2019 he won the US indoor title in the 2 mile, 8:25, meet record. Then that summer he finished 5th at USAs in the 5000. And then the injuries started. He tore his plantar fascia, he had foot problems, he had to pull out of the World Championships in 2019. And then the big one, he missed the Olympic Trials for Tokyo completely 'cause of injury. He actually said he felt like he had that taken away from him. And around that same time Tinman Elite split with their coach, and his mom ended up takin' over as his coach.

## Part 6
Now here go the part that hurt. 2024 Olympic Trials, the 10,000 meters. Top 3 go to Paris. And Drew Hunter finish 4th. 4th. 1 spot. That's how close he came to finally bein' an Olympian. Then 2025 USAs, the 5000, he finish 4th again. So every time it's time to get the job done on the track, he right there, but he can't get over the hump. And honestly that's what make this story so frustratin', 'cause he not bad, he really good, but when you had the hype he had in high school, really good ain't what people was expectin'. People expected him to be the guy runnin' in Olympic finals by now.

## Part 7
But I can't call him washed either, 'cause this year he just did somethin' crazy. He won the US road 5K title, and then in September at the World Road Running Championships in Copenhagen he ran 13:14 and set the American record for the road 5K. And he wrote somethin' this year that stuck wit' me, he said "running careers are over in the blink of the eye", and he said he still got unfinished business on the track. And that Adidas deal he signed at 18, that was a 10 year deal, so that whole chapter is basically done now. He 29 now, and the window is closin'.

'Cause on paper he got the American record, but he still ain't got that Olympic team, and that's what people gon' remember.
