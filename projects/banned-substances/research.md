# Every Banned Substance in Track: fact-check and case research

Remake of "Every Banned Substance in TRACK Explained in 23 Minutes" (https://youtu.be/Rtcvy_lpo3M). The original transcript was pulled with vidIQ on 2026-11-09.

**Confidence labels**
- **confirmed**: several reliable sources agree, or the official decision was found.
- **likely**: one decent source (often the Wikipedia athletics doping list or a single news report). Check before putting it on screen.
- **unsure**: a lead only. Don't use it without checking.

**Status labels**
- **PROVISIONAL**: the athlete is suspended but no final decision exists yet. Say "provisionally suspended", not "banned".

Master list used for cross-checks: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_doping_cases_in_athletics (L1 below).

## 2. EPO (incl. CERA / darbepoetin)
**What it does:** It is a hormone (synthetic copies of the kidney hormone erythropoietin) that makes bone marrow produce more red blood cells. That raises the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity and endurance. CERA (Mircera) and darbepoetin (Aranesp) are long-acting versions.

| Athlete | Country / event | Year | What happened | Ban | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rashid Ramzi | BHR, 1500m | 2008 (retest 2009) | Beijing samples were retested for **CERA** and came back positive. He was stripped of the 1500m Olympic gold, which went to Asbel Kiprop. | 2 yr | confirmed |
| Alex Schwazer | ITA, 50 km walk | 2012 | Defending Olympic champion. Tested positive for EPO before London and was expelled from the Games. Admitted it ("I wanted to be stronger"). He returned and got an 8-yr ban in 2016 for steroids. | 3 yr 9 mo (45 mo; sources vary 42–45) | confirmed |
| Rita Jeptoo | KEN, marathon | 2014 | rEPO in a pre-Chicago 2014 sample. Lost her 2014 Boston and Chicago wins. CAS doubled the ban. | 4 yr | confirmed |
| Asbel Kiprop | KEN, 1500m | 2017 test, 2019 ruling | Olympic champion (2008, upgraded) and 3x world champion. EPO found out of competition. | 4 yr | confirmed |
| Peres Jepchirchir | KEN, marathon | Oct 2026 | Tokyo 2020 Olympic and world marathon champion. **PROVISIONAL** suspension for EPO; the sample was taken before her Sydney Marathon 2026 win. | pending | confirmed (provisional) |
| Albert Korir | KEN, marathon | 2026 | 2021 NYC Marathon and 2025 Ottawa winner. **CERA** positive. Admitted it. | 5 yr | confirmed |

**Darbepoetin:** no track case found. The best-known sports case is not track: Johann Mühlegg (Spain, cross-country skiing), who tested positive for darbepoetin at Salt Lake City 2002 and was stripped of 3 golds.

## 4. THG (tetrahydrogestrinone, "the clear")
**What it does:** It is a designer anabolic steroid, chemically modified from gestrinone/trenbolone so that 2003-era tests couldn't see it. It works like other potent steroids.

| Athlete | Country / event | Year | What happened | Ban | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dwain Chambers | GBR, 100m | 2003 | Tested positive for THG out of competition. His 2002 European 100m title and his records were annulled. He later admitted THG, EPO, HGH and more. The BOA lifetime Olympic ban was overturned at CAS in 2012. | 2 yr | confirmed |
| Marion Jones | USA, 100/200/LJ | 2007 | **Never failed a test.** She admitted in Oct 2007 to using "the clear" in 2000–01. Forfeited all 5 Sydney medals (3 gold, 2 bronze) and served 6 months in jail for lying to federal agents. | 2 yr | confirmed |
| Regina Jacobs | USA, 1500m | 2003 | Multiple US champion. THG positive at the 2003 US Championships. | 4 yr | confirmed |
| Kevin Toth / Melissa Price | USA, shot / hammer | 2003 | THG positives from the retest of US Championships samples. | 2 yr each | likely |

**How THG was exposed:** In June 2003 a coach anonymously mailed a used syringe to USADA. The coach was later confirmed as **Trevor Graham**, who coached Jones, Montgomery and Gatlin. Don Catlin's UCLA lab identified THG from the syringe and built a test, and stored samples were then retested. Graham was banned for life in 2008.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwain_Chambers · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Jones · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Jacobs · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Graham · https://www.arabnews.com/node/254282 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BALCO_scandal

## 5. Nandrolone
**What it does:** It is an injectable anabolic steroid (19-nortestosterone) that builds muscle mass and strength. The idea that it eases joint pain is gym folklore and largely unproven in humans. The body makes trace amounts naturally; WADA's threshold for its marker, 19-norandrosterone, is 2 ng/mL.

| Athlete | Country / event | Year | What happened | Ban | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linford Christie | GBR, 100m | 1999 | 1992 Olympic champion. Positive at an indoor meet in Dortmund. UK Athletics cleared him; the IAAF banned him anyway. | 2 yr | confirmed |
| Dieter Baumann | GER, 5000m | 1999 | 1992 Olympic 5000m champion. Claimed his toothpaste had been spiked. Missed Sydney 2000. | 2 yr | confirmed |
| Merlene Ottey | JAM, 100/200m | 1999 | Positive in 1999. Jamaica, and then the IAAF, cleared her in 2000 and she ran in Sydney. Use her as an example of the "natural fluke" arguments. | cleared | confirmed |
| Shelby Houlihan | USA, 1500/5000m | 2020 test, 2021 CAS | US record holder. Blamed a pork offal burrito; CAS rejected that. Missed Tokyo and Paris. | 4 yr | confirmed |

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linford_Christie · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Baumann · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlene_Ottey · https://www.si.com/olympics/2021/06/15/shelby-houlihan-doping-ban-nandrolone-olympic-trials-positive-test-explained

## 6. Dianabol / methandienone (+ East German State Plan 14.25)
**What it does:** It is an oral anabolic steroid (methandrostenolone), developed in the US by Ciba in 1958. It gives fast gains in strength and mass, with water retention. It is liver-toxic.

| Athlete | Country / event | Year | What happened | Ban | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ivan Tsikhan | BLR, hammer | 2004 (retest 2012) | Athens 2004 silver stripped. | 2 yr | likely |
| Iryna Yatchenko | BLR, discus | 2004 (retest 2012) | Athens 2004 bronze stripped. | 2 yr | likely |
| Jason Livingston | GBR, 100m | 1992 | Sent home from the Barcelona Olympics. | 4 yr | likely |
| Janine Whitlock | GBR, pole vault | 2002 | British record holder. Positive at the Commonwealth Games trials. | 2 yr | confirmed |

- **Recent (2020–26):** no famous track case found. The brief suggested "metandienone in Kenyan cases", but I couldn't source it. Recent Kenyan steroid cases are mostly methasterone (Mark Otieno, Tokyo 2020), testosterone and norandrosterone. Don't claim metandienone for Kenya without a source.
- **East Germany:** State Plan 14.25 (1974) ran mainly on **Oral-Turinabol** (chlorodehydromethyltestosterone), a little **blue** pill made by state-owned VEB Jenapharm. Coaches handed it to athletes, including minors, as "vitamins". Estimates are "up to 10,000" athletes, with some sources saying 15,000, across all sports, not only track. The best-known track victim is shot putter Heidi Krieger (now Andreas Krieger).

Sources: L1 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janine_Whitlock · https://www.dawn.com/news/49234 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_in_East_Germany · https://www.pbs.org/wnet/secrets/the-state-sponsored-doping-program/52 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorodehydromethyltestosterone · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Krieger

## 7. Clenbuterol
**What it does:** It is a long-acting beta-2 agonist bronchodilator (asthma drug in some countries, vet drug in livestock). It raises metabolic rate and fat loss. In animals it has muscle-sparing and anabolic effects; human evidence is thin. It is also a known **meat contamination** risk in Mexico and China.

## 9. Meldonium (Mildronate)
**What it does:** It is a Latvian anti-ischaemic heart drug. It blocks carnitine synthesis, shifting the heart and muscle from burning fat toward burning glucose, which needs less oxygen per unit of energy. Evidence that it improves performance is weak, but WADA found widespread use. Banned from **1 Jan 2016** (confirmed).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Endeshaw Negesse | ETH, marathon | 2016 | 2015 Tokyo Marathon winner. One of several Ethiopians who tested positive in early 2016. | final ban not verified | confirmed (positive) |
| Ahmed Abdelwahed | ITA, steeplechase | 2022 test, Dec 2023 ruling | European silver medallist. | 4 yr | likely |

**Best-known meldonium case (not track):** Maria Sharapova, tennis, 2016. 2 yr, cut to 15 months.

## 11. Testosterone
**What it does:** It is the main male androgen. Taking extra builds muscle, strength and recovery. Labs catch it with the T/E ratio (above 4:1 triggers follow-up) plus carbon-isotope (IRMS) testing, which shows whether the testosterone is synthetic.

## 15. Captagon / fenethylline
**What it does:** It is a "codrug": the body breaks it down into amphetamine plus theophylline, a stimulant. The transcript gets this part right. It was developed by Degussa in 1961 as a **medicine** (ADHD, narcolepsy, depression), not for sport. Legal production ended in the 1980s; today it is an illicit drug in the Middle East.

- **Track:** **no documented track & field case found.**
- **Best-known sports case (not track):** German goalkeeper Harald "Toni" Schumacher wrote in his 1987 book *Anpfiff* that Captagon was common in the Bundesliga. Two former Eintracht Braunschweig team doctors later confessed to giving players Captagon in the 1970s and '80s. Nobody was sanctioned.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenethylline · https://germanfootball.co.uk/2022/05/31/when-toni-schumacher-exposed-doping-in-german-football/

## 16. IGF-1
**What it does:** It is the hormone that carries out most of HGH's growth effects in tissues. It drives cell growth and protein synthesis. Muscle "hyperplasia" (making new fibres) is shown in animals, not proven in humans.

- **Track:** **no sanctioned track & field case found.** It is hard to detect and is mostly caught through blood-profile monitoring.
- **Best-known sports case (not track):** golfer Vijay Singh admitted using deer-antler spray containing IGF-1 in 2013. He was cleared after WADA changed its guidance on the spray.
- **BALCO claim:** the original's line that BALCO stacked IGF-1 with HGH **could not be verified.** The documented BALCO products were THG, "the cream" (testosterone/epitestosterone), EPO, HGH, insulin and modafinil.

Sources: https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/igf-1-and-the-world-anti-doping-agency-prohibited-list/ · https://www.golfchannel.com/news/report-vijay-singh-admits-using-banned-substance · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BALCO_scandal

## 17. Salbutamol (albuterol)
**What it does:** It is a short-acting beta-2 agonist reliever inhaler that opens the airways. It is allowed inhaled up to 1,600 µg per 24 h (max 600 µg per 8 h) **with no TUE needed**. A urine level above 1,000 ng/mL is presumed to be an adverse finding. High oral doses have some anabolic effect.

## 18. Strychnine

## 20. Cannabis / THC
**What it does:** It is a psychoactive drug. It has no proven performance benefit and may relieve anxiety and pain. It is banned **in competition only**, above 150 ng/mL. Since 2021 it is a "Substance of Abuse": 3 months, or 1 month with treatment. WADA's rule for banning something is that it meets 2 of 3 criteria (enhances performance, risks health, violates the spirit of sport). The transcript gets that right.

## Errors and shaky claims in the original video

Ordered by importance.

1. **East German "State Plan 14.25 gave 10,000 athletes Dianabol": WRONG DRUG.** The program ran on **Oral-Turinabol** from state-owned Jenapharm. The "small blue pills" were Oral-Turinabol. Dianabol is an American (Ciba) drug. The 10,000 figure is an estimate, with some sources saying 15,000, and it covers all sports.
2. **Oxandrolone "key weapon of the East German machine, given to thousands": unsupported.** The documented GDR drugs are Oral-Turinabol, mestanolone (STS 646) and testosterone esters. Better oxandrolone story: Bilonoh's Athens gold being stripped.
3. **Captagon "a drug once designed for the racetrack": false.** It was a 1961 pharmaceutical for ADHD, narcolepsy and depression. There is also no track case, so it is weak as a "track" segment.
4. **Montgomery / HGH:** HGH was **not** "the centerpiece" of BALCO; THG and "the cream" were. Montgomery never failed a test: he was banned on documentary evidence (CAS 2005) and admitted HGH in 2008. The Victor Conte quote about HGH being more valuable for recovery is unverified.
5. **IGF-1 "used in BALCO, stacked with HGH": not verified.** Hyperplasia in humans is not proven.
6. **Marion Jones:** "forfeit all five Sydney medals" is **correct** (3 gold, 2 bronze). But she was not caught by a THG test; she **admitted** it in 2007 after lying to federal agents, and she went to jail.
7. **"A rival coach anonymously mailed a syringe": mostly right, but the label is wrong.** It was **Trevor Graham**, Jones's and Montgomery's own former coach, not a rival. He sent a used syringe in June 2003, and UCLA's lab decoded THG from it.
8. **EPO "so they invented the biological passport":** this implies EPO couldn't be tested. A direct urine EPO test existed from **2000 (Sydney)**. The ABP came later (cycling 2008, athletics 2009) to catch micro-dosing.
9. **Lashmanova:** the GW1516 bust (2014) is correct, but the video omits that she **lost the 2012 Olympic gold in 2022**, through Moscow-lab data rather than the GW1516 test.
10. **Salbutamol "athletes with a TUE can use it":** outdated. Inhaled salbutamol within the dose limits needs **no TUE**; the issue is the urine threshold (1,000 ng/mL).
11. **Cardarine "aerobic engine without running a single extra mile":** overstated. In the key 2008 mouse study GW1516 boosted endurance **with** training. The cancer claim is broadly correct: GSK dropped it after rodent cancers, in mice and rats, and WADA warned about it in 2013.
12. **Meldonium "same energy with way less oxygen":** overstated. Performance benefit in humans is unproven. The **1 Jan 2016** date is correct.
13. **Nandrolone "actively soothes and repairs your joints":** folklore, not established medicine. "The body produces tiny amounts" is **correct**.
14. **Amphetamine:** "used since the 1930s" is a stretch; the drug was marketed in the 1930s, but documented sports use is mainly from the 1940s and '50s. "Shaves fractions off reaction time" is unsupported.
15. **GHRP-6 "weapon of choice for sophisticated doping programs":** unsupported; there is no notable track case. A short detection window (around 1–2 days for the parent drug in urine) and the hunger side effect are **correct**.
16. **Diuretics "presence is basically an admission of guilt":** overstated. Many cases are contamination or medication claims, such as Chepngetich and Langat. Banned at all times is **correct**.
17. **Cannabis "the perfect cover story" theory:** the video's own speculation; no source. Presenting it as what "insiders believe" is unsupported. Richardson's facts are **correct**.
