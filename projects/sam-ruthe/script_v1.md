# Sam Ruthe: script v1 (26 Nov 2026). Rise, then the injury, then the comeback question. Cloned voice (A3b).
Working title: "He's 16 And Runs A 3:48 Mile... How?"

## Part 1
Most kids in New Zealand are worried about school at 16, and this kid flew 50 hours to Boston, ran his first race ever on an indoor track, and beat a bunch of grown professionals in 3:48.88 for the mile. His name is Sam Ruthe, and honestly I don't think y'all understand how crazy that time is, 'cause Jakob Ingebrigtsen, the guy everybody calls the best miler alive, couldn't even run that at 17. So I wanna go through how this kid got here, 'cause the story is way deeper than one race, and the way his year ended is not how anybody saw it going.

## Part 2
Sam is from Tauranga, a beach town on the North Island of New Zealand, and running is basically in his blood. His dad Ben was a national record holder in the 800 and the 1000, his mom Jess won national cross country titles, and his grandma Rosemary won the 800 at the 1970 Commonwealth Games running for Scotland, then went to the Olympics 2 years later. His dad said he remembers Sam running laps around the tent at the beach before he was even 2 years old. But I really respect this part, his parents didn't let him train for real until he was 13. No crazy mileage, nobody pushing him, they just let him be a kid.

## Part 3
When he finally did start, his coach Craig Kirkwood put him in a group with Sam Tanner, a 2-time Olympian, and that's who he been training with ever since. And it didn't take long. In February 2025, at 15, he ran 3:41.25 in the 1500 and broke Jakob Ingebrigtsen's world best for a 15-year-old. A month later at nationals he ran with his training partner, the Olympian, all the way to the line, and they literally tied, both 3:44.31, so they had to share the title. Then on March 19, 2025, in Auckland, with Tanner pacing him, he ran the mile in 3:58.35. That made him the youngest person in history to break 4 minutes in the mile, younger than Jakob was and younger than anybody ever. He was 25 days away from turning 16.

## Part 4
A month after that Nike signed him. And instead of moving somewhere and changing everything, he stayed home in Tauranga, stayed in school and stayed with the same coach. Then at the end of 2025 he just kept stacking records. He ran 3:38.62 in the 1500 at the national high school champs, and that was in a semi-final, and it broke the schools record by like 8 seconds. Then in January he beat Sam Tanner in the 800 in 1:45.86, and a week before Boston he ran 3:53.83 for the mile, the fastest mile ever by a 16-year-old.

## Part 5
Then came Boston. January 31, 2026, at Boston University. He flew in 3 days before, and he had never raced on an indoor track in his life. The plan was to sit behind Tanner, but Tanner's leg tightened up and he had to drop out, so now this 16-year-old is out there by himself with grown pros, and he was 3 years younger than anybody else in the race. With a lap to go he went right around Pieter Sisk, a Belgian Olympian, closed his last lap in 28 seconds, and won it in 3:48.88. That's a world under-18 best, and it broke Sir John Walker's New Zealand record that had been sitting there for 44 years. John Walker is an Olympic champion, and a 16-year-old took his record.

## Part 6
2 weeks later they put him in a race with Cole Hocker, the Olympic 1500 champion, in North Carolina, and this is where he found out what the pros are really like. Hocker ran 3:45 and set the American indoor record, and Sam got boxed in, stuck out in lane 2, and finished 7th. Some people online said he was overhyped, and honestly I think that's crazy, 'cause he still ran 3:52 at 16 in a bad race. And the way he talked about it after was so mature. He said it's more of a contact sport over there and you gotta be more brutal. Then he went back to Boston and ran 7:43 in the 3000, and when he got home he won his second national title in the 1500 by more than 6 seconds.

## Part 7
Everything was lined up. He got named to his first New Zealand team for the World Under-20 Championships, he was entered in the Bowerman Mile at the Pre Classic against Hocker and Cam Myers, and the Commonwealth Games were in Glasgow, Scotland, the same country his grandma won gold for. His dad even said Sam would love to do it for his nan. And then in April he got a stress fracture in his fibula, and it wasn't even from running, he got hurt playing soccer. Just like that, the whole season was gone.

## Part 8
And this is the part that I think tells you who this kid really is. He could have rushed back. Instead he started filming his own YouTube series called Back on Track, showing the crutches, the swimming and the bike, and he said he biked a little over 9 hours in 3 days. In May he pulled himself out of the Commonwealth Games, and in June he pulled out of World Under-20s too, 'cause he didn't wanna show up not ready. His coach said you wouldn't want to be standing on that start line not ready. And then he made an even bigger decision, he's leaving school at the end of this year to run full time, with a plan to base in Europe in 2027.

## Part 9
So where does that leave him? He's 17 now, his first run back was in June after 8 weeks off, and the New Zealand summer season is about to start. And look, I've covered a lot of prodigies on this channel that didn't make it, so I'm not gon' sit here and say it's guaranteed. But this one feels different to me, 'cause his people have been protecting him since he was a little kid. He'll be 18 at the 2027 World Championships and 19 at the LA Olympics. So y'all tell me, when Sam Ruthe comes back, is he gon' be the next Jakob, or did this injury slow him down for good? Let me know in the comments.
