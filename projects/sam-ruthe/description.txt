Sam Ruthe was 15 when he became the youngest person ever to run a sub-4 mile, and 16 when he flew 50 hours to Boston, ran his first race ever on an indoor track and clocked 3:48.88, breaking Sir John Walker's 44-year-old New Zealand record. Then a stress fracture took his whole season. This is how a kid from Tauranga became the fastest teenage miler the world has seen, and what comes next.

So y'all tell me: when Sam Ruthe comes back, is he the next Jakob? Let me know in the comments.

Facts: born April 12, 2009, Tauranga, New Zealand; coached by Craig Kirkwood, trains with 2-time Olympian Sam Tanner. Grandmother Rosemary Wright won 800m gold for Scotland at the 1970 Commonwealth Games. 2025: 3:41.25 1500m at 15 (beat Jakob Ingebrigtsen's world age-15 best of 3:42.44); dead heat with Tanner for the NZ 1500m title (3:44.31); 3:58.35 mile on March 19, 2025 in Auckland, the youngest sub-4 ever; signed with Nike in April 2025; 3:38.62 1500m at the NZ Secondary Schools champs (Dec 2025). 2026: 1:45.86 800m beating Tanner; 3:53.83 mile, fastest ever by a 16-year-old (Jan 24); 3:48.88 mile at the BU Terrier Classic (Jan 31), world U18 best and NZ record, breaking John Walker's 3:49.08 (1982); 7th in 3:52.46 behind Cole Hocker's 3:45.94 American record (Feb 14); 7:43.16 3000m (Feb 22); 2nd NZ 1500m title (March 7). April 2026: fibula stress fracture (from playing football); withdrew from the Commonwealth Games and the World U20 Championships; leaving school at the end of 2026 to run full time.
