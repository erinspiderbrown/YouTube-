## Part 1
So the NCAA cross country championships are tomorrow, and there's something about the men's race that I don't think enough people are talking about. A Kenyan man has not won NCAA cross country since 2019. That's 6 straight years.

And that's crazy to me, 'cause before that the Kenyans were running this race. From 2012 to 2019 they won it 5 out of 8 times. Edward Cheserek from Oregon won it 3 years in a row. Then 2019, Edwin Kurgat from Iowa State wins it, and since then it's been Conner Mantz from BYU twice, then Charles Hicks from Stanford, then Graham Blanks from Harvard twice, and last year Habtom Samuel from New Mexico, and he's from Eritrea, not Kenya.

## Part 2
Now a lot of people say it's the weather. Nationals is in November, so it's usually cold. Like half of these races since 2013 been in the 20s and 30s. In 2018 in Madison it straight up snowed on the course. 2016 in Terre Haute it was 36 degrees with the wind blowing, and that's the year Cheserek was going for his 4th title in a row and he finished 3rd.

But I looked into it, and honestly the weather thing don't really hold up like I thought it would. 'Cause the coldest, nastiest races in Terre Haute, the Kenyans won 'em. 2013 the wind chill was in the teens and Cheserek still won. 2019 it was 36 degrees, raining, mud everywhere, and Kurgat won. And they lost some races when it was 60 degrees and sunny too.

So whatever the reason is, tomorrow nobody gets to use the weather as an excuse, 'cause the forecast in Terre Haute is sunny and about 57 degrees when they go off. No rain, no snow, the course should be dry. It's the first time they've been back in Terre Haute since 2019, and it's gonna be like 20 degrees warmer than that day.

## Part 3
And the guy everybody's looking at is Rocky Hansen. He runs for Wake Forest, he's from North Carolina, and he was the first high schooler in North Carolina to ever run under 4 minutes in the mile.

Last year at nationals he went straight to the front from the gun and almost pulled it off. He finished 2nd, only 4.1 seconds behind Samuel. Then this summer he got 2nd at NCAA outdoors in the 5000, behind Samuel again. Track & Field News had him as their number 1 guy coming into this season, and he was the only American in their top 10.

## Part 4
But here's what got me a little worried about him. He only raced twice this fall, and he didn't win either one. He was 4th at Nuttycombe, then 2nd at the ACC. And he skipped regionals. So we really don't know what kinda shape he's in.

And Samuel ain't even there this year. He went pro with HOKA. So the guy who beat Hansen twice is gone, but now there's a whole group of Kenyans coming for that spot.

## Part 5
You got Elsingi Kipruto from Louisville. He's the one that beat Hansen at the ACC, and then he won the Southeast Regional in 28:20. Oklahoma State got 2 of 'em, Brian Musau, who won the Midwest Regional, and Denis Kipngetich, who won the Big 12. Solomon Kipchoge from Washington State finished 3rd at nationals last year and just won the West Regional. And New Mexico, the number 1 team in the country, been running their guys as a pack all season.

Some people are saying this is the most wide open this race has been in a long time.

## Part 6
So for me, it really comes down to this. If Rocky Hansen is healthy and he runs from the front like he did last year, I think he got a real shot at winning this thing as an American. But if he's still banged up, I think a Kenyan wins this for the first time since 2019, and this time it's gonna be in good weather.
