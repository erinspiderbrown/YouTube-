Quincy Wilson was 16 when he became the youngest American man ever to run track at the Olympics, and he came home from Paris with a gold medal. He broke a 42-year-old high school record twice in 2 days, then ran 44.10 at 17. But in 2026 a kid from his own county, Jayden DeLeon, beat him for the world U20 title. Is Quincy Wilson still the future of the 400?

CHAPTERS_TBD

So y'all tell me: is Quincy Wilson gonna come through and shine in LA, or is Jayden DeLeon about to take his spot? Let me know in the comments.

Facts: born January 8, 2008 in New London, CT; military family (dad Roy, Navy); Bullis School, coach Joe Lee. 47.59 at 14 (July 2022, broke Obea Moore's 14-and-under record). New Balance NIL deal at 15. 2024 Olympic Trials: 44.66 (round 1, broke Darrell Robinson's 44.69 from 1982) and 44.59 (semi); 6th in the final. Paris 2024: youngest US male track Olympian (16 years, 214 days); led off the 4x400 heat (47.27); Team USA won the final in 2:54.43 (Olympic record). 2025: 43.99 anchor at the Penn Relays (Bullis 3:06.31, national HS record, 2nd to Kingston College); 44.10 in Memphis on July 12 (HS and U18 world record); 4th in his USATF semi (45.39); committed to Maryland (coach Andrew Valmon) in November. 2026: beat Jayden DeLeon 45.37 to 45.38 at NB Indoor Nationals; lost to him 44.52 to 44.84 at USATF U20s; silver at the World U20 Championships in Eugene in 44.62 (DeLeon 44.47, championship record); 4x400 gold together; Bullis won the Penn Relays Championship of America (3:10.15), first US boys win in 19 years; 7th in 45.53 in Memphis.
