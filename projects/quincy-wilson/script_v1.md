# Quincy Wilson: script v1 (user reads it; 27 Nov 2026)
Teleprompter parts are "Part 1, 2...". Digits for numbers. Facts: research.md.

## Part 1
Quincy Wilson is one of those names where if you follow track even a little bit, you already know it. 16 years old, running at the Olympics, the youngest American man ever to run track at the Olympics, and he came home with a gold medal. And honestly when that happened I really felt like this kid was gonna be the next big thing in the 400, like we was watching the start of something crazy. But it's been 2 years now, and this summer a kid from right down the street in Maryland beat him for a world title. So I wanna get into it, is Quincy Wilson still the future of the 400, or did everybody crown him way too early?

## Part 2
Quincy was born January 8, 2008 in New London, Connecticut, and he's a military kid. His dad Roy played football at the Naval Academy and served in the Navy, so they moved around a lot, Kansas, Florida, Georgia, Virginia, and finally Maryland. His mom Monique played college basketball and soccer, and his big sister Kadence ran in college too, so this whole family is athletes. And they actually moved to Maryland so he could go to Bullis, a private school right outside D.C., and run for coach Joe Lee.

## Part 3
He started running when he was 8, and by 14 he was already breaking records that been sitting there for decades. In July 2022 he ran 47.59 in the 400, and that broke Obea Moore's 14-and-under record that was 30 years old. Then at 15 New Balance signed him to a NIL deal. So before he even got his driver's license, he already had a shoe deal.

## Part 4
Then come the 2024 Olympic Trials, and this is where everybody found out who he was. He's 16, running against grown men, and in the first round he runs 44.66. That broke Darrell Robinson's high school record, 44.69, a record from 1982. 42 years. Then 2 days later he went out and broke it again, 44.59. He made the final and finished 6th, and his coach Joe Lee was in tears after, saying a month before that Quincy had literally been in the hospital sick. And what's crazy is, 6th place usually means you're going home.

## Part 5
But Team USA put him in the relay pool. And his coach really pranked him with a fake bad news call, telling him he unfortunately was gonna have to go to Paris. So in August 2024, at 16 years and 214 days, he became the youngest American man ever to run track at the Olympics, passing Jim Ryun. He led off the 4x400 in the first round and split 47.27, which was one of the slowest legs in that heat, and people went at him for that. Later he said his hamstring grabbed up on him in that race and he tried to smile it off. Team USA ran the final without him and won in 2:54.43, an Olympic record, and since he ran the heat, he got a gold medal too.

## Part 6
Now 2025 is where he really started to look like the real deal. At the Penn Relays he anchored Bullis in 43.99. That's a high schooler splitting under 44. They ran 3:06.31, a national high school record, but they still lost to Kingston College from Jamaica. Then in May he lost a 400 to Jayden Horton-Mims, and that's the first time people started asking if he already hit his ceiling. And at New Balance Nationals people was yelling another kid's name at him while he was in the blocks, and he still went out there and won.

## Part 7
And then July 12, 2025 in Memphis, he ran 44.10. That's the high school record, and the under-18 world record, and to this day that's still his best. But 3 weeks later at USAs, the senior championships, he finished 4th in his semi in 45.39 and didn't make the final, so he missed the World Championships in Tokyo. And in November he made his college decision. Texas A&M, South Carolina, USC, and UCLA all wanted him, and he picked Maryland, to stay home and run for Andrew Valmon, who won Olympic gold in the 4x400 himself.

## Part 8
Which brings us to 2026, and this is the part that got everybody talking. There's another kid in Maryland named Jayden DeLeon, he went to Montgomery Blair, same county as Bullis, and he signed with LSU. In March at New Balance Indoor Nationals, Quincy beat him by 9 thousandths of a second, 45.37 to 45.38. But in June at the U20 championships, DeLeon got him, 44.52 to 44.84. And then in August, at the World U20 Championships in Eugene, DeLeon won gold in 44.47, a championship record, and Quincy got silver in 44.62. So for the first time, Quincy Wilson was not the best 400 runner in his own state.

## Part 9
Now to be fair, it wasn't all bad. 2 days after that, him and DeLeon ran on the same 4x400 and won gold together, and Quincy led it off. And at the Penn Relays this year Bullis won the Championship of America in 3:10.15, the first time an American boys team won that race in 19 years, and he anchored it. That was the one thing he said he hadn't done yet in high school. But he never ran faster than that 44.10 this year. He even went back to the same meet in Memphis where he ran it, and finished 7th in 45.53.

## Part 10
So here's my take. I don't think Quincy Wilson is washed or anything like that, he ran 44.10 at 17, and a lot of grown men never run that in their life. Every prodigy hits a wall at some point, and a 400 runner going from 17 to 18 ain't always gonna drop time every year. What I do think is the pressure is real now. He got the gold medal, the shoe deal, the hype, and now he got a kid from down the street who beat him for a world title. I really feel like this next year at Maryland is gonna tell us a lot.

## Part 11
And the big one is LA 2028. He'll be 20, and he said himself he wants the whole city behind him going into 2028. So I wanna hear from y'all. Is Quincy Wilson gonna come through and shine in LA, or is Jayden DeLeon about to take his spot? Let me know in the comments.
