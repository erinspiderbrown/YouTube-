# Michael Norman: script v2 (user reads it; user's angle 24 Nov 2026: great prospect, went to college, wasted talent)
Title: "The Tragic Downfall Of Michael Norman" (user, 24 Nov 2026)
Read it how you talk. Numbers are written the way you'd say them.

## Part 1
Michael Norman might be the biggest wasted talent in American sprinting. This dude opened up a season running 43.45 in the 400, and people was really talkin' about him breakin' the world record. And now he 28, he finished dead last in the Olympic final in Paris, he missed a whole year with surgery, and this summer he couldn't even make the final at USAs. So how do you go from the next world record holder to not even top 8 in America? Let's get into it.

## Part 2
Y'all gotta understand how good this man was comin' out of high school. 2016 Olympic Trials, the 200 meter final, and there's two high schoolers in it. Noah Lyles and Michael Norman. Lyles finished 4th and broke the high school record, and Norman was right behind him in 5th running 20.14. Two kids from high school runnin' with grown men for an Olympic spot. And the year before that at the California state meet Norman ran 45.19 in the 400, which tied the national high school record, and he ran 7 races in like 28 hours. So both of them could've went pro right out of high school.

## Part 3
And here go the difference. Noah Lyles turned pro right out of high school. Michael Norman went to college. He went to USC, and honestly I can't even knock him for that, 'cause he did really well. In 2018 he ran 44.52 indoors, which was the fastest indoor 400 ever. Then at NCAA outdoors he ran 43.61, a collegiate record and the fastest time in the world that year. He won the Bowerman, which is basically the Heisman for track. Then he signed with Nike and went pro. On paper, everything was goin' exactly how it was supposed to go.

## Part 4
And then 2019 happened. His very first 400 of the year at Mt. SAC he runs 43.45. As a season opener. That's one of the fastest times ever run, and at the time only a few men in history had ever gone faster. Then he ran 19.70 in the 200, and the next year 9.86 in the 100. Under 10, under 20, under 44, and the only other man who had done that at the time was Wayde van Niekerk, the world record holder. So everybody was sayin' it, the world record is comin', it's just a matter of time.

## Part 5
And then the first big choke. 2019 World Championships in Doha, he's the favorite for gold, and in the semifinal he basically shuts it down with about 100 to go and jogs it in dead last. Didn't even make the final. His coach said it was a tendon injury. Then Tokyo 2021, the Olympics. He makes the final, he's leading, and he fades to 5th in 44.31. No medal. And that 43.45 he ran in 2019? He never ran that fast again. So at this point the best season opener we ever seen turned into zero individual medals at the two biggest meets.

## Part 6
Now 2022, I know what y'all gon' say, he won the world title. And he did. He won Worlds in Eugene in 44.29. But I really feel like we gotta keep it real about that race. 44.29 was the slowest winning time at Worlds since 2011, and Kirani James was right there behind him in 44.48. That's the Olympic champ from 2012, only 2 tenths behind him. So yeah he got the gold, but it wasn't the dominant run people expected from a guy who opened up in 43.45. It was one of the slowest years and he still barely got the job done.

## Part 7
And instead of building off that, 2023 he does somethin' nobody saw comin'. He switches to the 100 meters. He leaves his coach Quincy Watts, goes to John Smith, and at USAs he goes out in the first round of the 100. He didn't even run the 400 at Worlds that year, even though as defending champ he already had a spot. He just shut it down. So the world champion in the 400 basically walked away from the 400 for a whole year.

## Part 8
Then he comes back for the Olympic year. 2024 Olympic Trials he finishes 2nd in 44.41 and makes the team. But he said before Trials he hadn't really sprinted all out since February 'cause of injuries. And in Paris it all caught up with him. He made the final and finished 8th. Dead last. 45.62. And meanwhile Noah Lyles, the other high schooler from that 2016 Trials, won the Olympic gold in the 100 that same week. Then Norman didn't race again for about a year and a half. In 2025 he had surgery, and from what he said it was a pelvis injury that got worse while he was tryna make Paris.

## Part 9
This year he came back with a new coach, he won some smaller races, he won the 400 at the LA Track Festival, so it looked like he might be back. But then at USAs in July he wasn't even in the final. The world champion from 2022 couldn't make the top 8 in his own country.

## Part 10
And that's why I call it a downfall, 'cause when you look at the talent, it's horrendous how it turned out. 43.45, 19.70, 9.86, he had the speed of a 100 guy and the strength of a 400 guy. And at the global championships he was last in Doha, 5th in Tokyo, a slow win in Eugene, skipped Budapest, and dead last in Paris. I'm not sayin' he ain't had injuries, he did, but at some point you gotta ask, was he ever gon' get the job done when it mattered?
