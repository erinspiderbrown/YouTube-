Title: "The Tragic Downfall Of Michael Norman" (user, 24 Nov 2026)
Read it how you talk. Numbers are written the way you'd say them.

## Part 1
Two years ago Michael Norman was the world champion in the 400, and honestly a lot of people had him as the guy who was gon' break the world record. Then in Paris he made the Olympic final and finished dead last. Then he disappeared for a whole year, had surgery, and this summer at USAs he wasn't even in the final. So how do you go from the best 400 runner on the planet to not even making the final in your own country? That's what I wanna get into, 'cause this one is really sad to me.

## Part 2
First y'all gotta understand how talented this dude was. Michael Norman went to Vista Murrieta High School in California, and in 2015 at the state meet he ran 45.19 in the 400, which tied the national high school record. Same meet he won the 200 too, and he ran 7 races in like 28 hours. The next year, still in high school, he made the Olympic Trials final in the 200 and beat Justin Gatlin in the semis to get there. Then he went to World Juniors and won the 200. I'm telling y'all, this kid was different.

## Part 3
Then he goes to USC and it gets crazy. March 2018 at NCAA indoors he runs 44.52, which was the fastest indoor 400 ever run, by anybody. Then that June at NCAA outdoors in Eugene he runs 43.61, a collegiate record and the fastest time in the world that year. He won the Bowerman, which is basically the Heisman for track. So he signed with Nike, turned pro, and his very first 400 of 2019 he goes out at Mt. SAC and runs 43.45. As a season opener. That's one of the fastest times in history, and people really thought Wayde van Niekerk 43.03 was in trouble.

And it wasn't just the 400. That same year he ran 19.70 in the 200 in Rome, and then in 2020 he ran 9.86 in the 100. So now he under 10, under 20 and under 44, and at that time the only other man in history who had done all three was Wayde van Niekerk, the world record holder. That's how special this dude was. He had the speed of a 100 guy and the strength of a 400 guy, and honestly I feel like that's why everybody thought the record was only a matter of time.

## Part 4
And then the first crack. 2019 World Championships in Doha. He's the favorite to win gold. And in the semifinal he basically just stops running with about 100 to go and jogs it in, dead last. Turns out he was hurt, his coach said it was a tendon injury. Then Tokyo come around in 2021, he makes the final, finishes 5th, but he did get a gold medal on the 4x4. So he got his Olympic gold, but not the one everybody expected.

## Part 5
But 2022 is when he finally came through. He won USAs, and then at the World Championships in Eugene, at home, he won the 400 in 44.29. World champion. He beat Kirani James, the 2012 Olympic champ, and he won it in front of a home crowd. Gold in the 4x4 too. At this point I really feel like everybody thought, ok, this is the start, he finally healthy, he finally put it together. And then he did something nobody saw coming.

## Part 6
In 2023 he switched to the 100 meters. He left his coach Quincy Watts and went to John Smith to try to be a 100 guy. And at USAs he went out in the first round of the 100. He didn't even run the 400 at Worlds that year, even though as the defending champ he had a spot already. He just shut it down. He talked about having setback after setback, tendonitis, all of that. So the world champion in the 400 basically walked away from the 400 for a whole year.

## Part 7
Then he came back to Quincy Watts and the 400 for the Olympic year. 2024 Olympic Trials he finishes 2nd in 44.41 and makes the team for Paris. But he said before Trials he hadn't really sprinted all out since February 'cause of injuries. And in Paris it all caught up with him. He made the final, and he finished 8th. Last. 45.62. The world champion from 2 years before, dead last in the Olympic final. And then he didn't race again for about a year and a half.

## Part 8
In 2025 he didn't race at all. His agent said he had surgery for something that happened the season before, and he spent most of the year recovering. And from what he said it was his pelvis, it got worse while he was trying to make Paris and run Paris. Then this year he came back, new coach, he won some races, he won the 400 at the LA Track Festival, so it looked like he was back. But then at USAs in July he wasn't in the final. The world champion from 2022 couldn't make the top 8 in America.

## Part 9
And that's what make this tragic to me, 'cause it ain't like he got caught doing nothing or he was lazy. His body just kept breaking down. On paper he still one of the most talented 400 runners we ever had. 43.45, world champion, Olympic gold on the relay. But he 28 now, and every year it's something.
