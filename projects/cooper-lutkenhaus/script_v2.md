## Part 1
This year a high school junior from Texas went overseas, won a world title, and was back in class the next week. His name is Cooper Lutkenhaus, he's 17, and he's already beaten the Olympic champion in the 800. I've been watching track my whole life and I really don't think we've ever seen a kid do what he's doing right now. So let me show y'all how he went from a freshman nobody knew to the best young runner in the world.

## Part 2
Cooper is from Justin, Texas, a small town right outside Fort Worth, and he runs for Northwest High School. He didn't even start running until 7th grade, and he was playing a bunch of other sports too. It wasn't until the summer before his freshman year that he decided track was gonna be his thing. And his dad George been coaching high school running for years, so he grew up around it.

## Part 3
And it didn't take long. As a freshman in 2024 he ran 1:47.58 and broke the Texas state record. As a freshman. Then his sophomore year he gets with a new coach, Chris Capeau, and it's like he jumped to another level. He broke the high school indoor record at Millrose. He won the state title. And then at Brooks PR in June 2025 he ran 1:46.26 and broke Michael Granville's national high school record, a record that been sitting there since 1996. 2 weeks later at Nike Nationals, in the pouring rain, he ran 1:45.45 and broke his own record.

## Part 4
But the moment that really put him on the map was August 2025, USAs in Eugene. He's 16, he's in the final with grown men, and with 200 to go he's sitting way back. Then he just starts passing people. The whole stadium is going crazy, and he runs 1:42.27 and finishes 2nd, right behind Donavan Brazier, the 2019 world champ, and ahead of Bryce Hoppel. That's an under-18 world record, and it made him the first 16-year-old American to ever make a World Championship team.

## Part 5
And right after that, Nike signs him. 16 years old, still in high school, and he goes pro. But he kept it simple. He stayed home in Texas, he kept training with his high school coach, and he kept going to class like a regular kid.

## Part 6

## Part 7

He did that over spring break, and then he went right back to school.

## Part 8
