## Part 1
Man, Jane Hedengren and Rocky Hansen got cooked at nationals, and I ain't gon' sugarcoat it. Jane came in as the favorite, she was the runner-up last year, everybody had her winning this thing, and she ended up 39th. Not top 10. Not top 20. 39th. And Rocky, he was the runner-up last year too, and he was leading this race with 2K to go, and he still ended up 6th. So I gotta ask it, are these two choke artists or what?

## Part 2
Let's start with Jane. Last year as a freshman she ran 18:38.9 at nationals, the fastest freshman time in meet history, and she was 2nd. This year she won Big 12, and even NCAA.com was saying she should still be considered the favorite. Now I know some people gon' try to make excuses for her. I ain't tryna hear it. She showed up. She toed the line as the favorite, and she got cooked. If you ain't ready, don't show up.

## Part 3
And it wasn't even close. Hannah Gapes from NC State, she's from New Zealand, took it out from the start and led basically the whole race, and she won in 18:44.3, a new course record. Jane ran 19:26.1. That's like 42 seconds behind. And it gets worse, she wasn't even the first BYU girl. Lexi Goff, Taylor Lovell, and Riley Chamberlain all beat her, so Jane was BYU's 4th runner. BYU came in ranked number 1 and they finished 3rd, and Alabama won the whole thing for the first time ever.

## Part 4
Now Rocky. Rocky Hansen from Wake Forest, he was 2nd last year behind Habtom Samuel, and he was one of the top guys coming back. At 8K he was out front leading the race. On paper that's where you wanna be. Then Elsingi Kipruto from Louisville, he from Kenya, he just took off over the last 2K, and Rocky couldn't go with him. Kipruto won in 28:27.7, a course record, and Rocky faded all the way back to 6th in 28:38.4. When you leading nationals with 2K to go, you gotta get the job done.

## Part 5
And here's what really got me. In the women's race the whole top 10 was international runners. The first American was Katy Zang from Indiana in 15th, and she was the only American in the top 20. The men's race was the same story, international runners all over the front. LetsRun even ran the numbers before the meet, and 45% of the scorers on the qualifying teams were international.

And the weather was perfect. Course records went down in both races, so you know it was fast. I really feel like when it's fast and dry like that, it plays right into their hands. The last time nationals was in the snow was 2018 in Madison, and an American won the women's race, Dani Jones from Colorado. So when it's a pure speed race on a good day, our people gotta come through, and they didn't.
